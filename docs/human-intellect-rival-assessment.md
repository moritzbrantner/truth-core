# Human intellect: rival assessment and the unmet inference

The results merged in #420, #422 and #427 do not yet exclude a physically
constituted thinker. The first two are conditional reductios with disputed
interference premises. The subsistence composition is explicitly a transcription.
This assessment supplies an affirmative rival source and a research target; it
adds no theorem, changes no statement meaning, and claims no premise reduction.

## The strongest local reply

Here is the proposed rival reconstruction, not a quotation or source claim:
mental activity is the activity of a physically constituted human being, with
mental and physical descriptions picking out the same causally effective
occurrences. A thought's object need not be a constitutive property of its
bearer. A bodily mechanism's own determinate nature therefore does not, without
an additional argument, imply a forbidden object in its representational range.
The positive replacement is identity or realization of mental causation, rather
than unexplained interaction by a second agent. Its unrestricted cognitive
scope and content correctness remain unexplained by the present witnesses.

This reply grants neither interference premise. It can provisionally grant
bodily determinacy and even unrestricted bodily-nature capacity to isolate the
disagreement. It disputes the move between them, rather than the reductio once
that move is assumed. Accepting a conditional theorem is not accepting its
premises. Equally, denying interference does not establish physicalism.

## Precisely what remains to prove

The earliest unmet step is still
`STMT-INTRINSIC-BODILY-NATURE-BLOCKS-UNIVERSAL-COGNITION`:

```text
∀ n, m.BodilyNature n → m.HasNaturally i n →
  ¬ (∀ k, m.BodilyNature k → m.CanUnderstand i k)
```

Its organ counterpart,
`STMT-ORGAN-BODILY-NATURE-BLOCKS-UNIVERSAL-COGNITION`, changes the bearer of
the constitutive nature to a bodily instrument related by `through i organ`.
Neither is a consequence of bodily determinacy alone. The existing omission
controls already establish that formal non-entailment; another primitive
one-point model would add no defense of actual representation.

Q.75 a.2 co. (31460) says naturally present features impede cognition of other
things, illustrating this with bitter taste and a colored vessel. To discharge
the displayed proposition, the next investigation must explain why the
limitation necessarily applies to every putative bodily intellectual principle,
not only those sensory mechanisms, and why alternative physical encoding,
compensation or organization cannot remove it. A finite machine demonstrating
many representational domains would not prove unrestricted capacity either.
The source asserts the inference; its illustrations do not themselves settle
this dispute. Do not silently replace “all bodily natures” with “universal
concepts”: q.75 a.5's reception argument discussed by Pasnau is a separate
possible investigation requiring its own Latin witness and interpretation.

The subsequent gaps remain
`STMT-NONORGANIC-INTELLECT-OPERATION-ATTRIBUTION` and
`STMT-PER-SE-INTELLECTUAL-OPERATION-REQUIRES-SUBSISTENCE`. A negative no-organ
capacity statement needs the explicit attribution premise to yield a positive
exercise witness. The operation/existence premise then supplies weak
non-inherence. Neither existing theorem specifies a neural-dependence relation,
a predicate for modern physical realization, or a causal-identity theory.
A bridge equating every modern physical realization with q.75 bodily
instrumentality would be an additional commitment requiring defense, not a
translation licensed by a shared word. No such bridge is added here.

If instrumental bodily exercise were independently excluded and the rival
were shown to require exactly that exercise, that particular realization would
be incompatible with the conclusion. As matters stand, applying the theorem
to a concrete physicalist account requires both its disputed premises and this
semantic comparison. Weak subsistence cannot alone settle the comparison by
renaming its bearer a nonphysical substance.

## Rival boundaries and rejected readings

These are working comparison categories, not newly attributed doctrines.
Reductive identity identifies mental occurrences or properties with physical
ones; nonreductive realization retains a further mental description or property
and owes an account of its efficacy. Functionalism specifies a role and needs
an additional substrate thesis to become physicalism. Eliminativism instead
revises or removes the target mental taxonomy and must explain the evidence
that motivated it. Naturalism is a broader program; monism counts fundamental
kinds without selecting the physical kind. None is refuted by naming another.

Hylomorphism here treats soul as form of this human composite while asserting
qualified subsistence. Substance dualism would posit a distinct mental
substance, a stronger conclusion than the present weak non-inherence.
Idealist monism would make the fundamental account mental; neutral monism
would make it neither intrinsically mental nor physical. These alternatives
also owe accounts of content, embodiment, unity and causal efficacy. The
consulted witnesses do not supply their complete answer maps. They remain
uninvestigated rivals, not discarded possibilities.

Rejected: reading Papineau's causal argument as a proof of every domain's
physicality; treating neural correlation as identity or as a refutation of
object-side dependence; treating Pasnau's illustration criticism as a complete
physicalist explanation; treating weak subsistence as Cartesian completeness;
calling an uninterpreted omission model a naturalistic cognitive implementation.
No inference to God or Catholicism is supplied.
