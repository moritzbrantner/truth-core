## Remaining semantic obligation

The following is a plan for analyst reconstruction, not an attribution to
Blackburn or Gibbard and not a verified result. A suitable model should make
sentences and their conditional construction explicit, distinguish descriptive
possibilities from norm systems or plans, and define acceptance of a sentence
by the possibilities compatible with a planning state. It should derive the
inference from acceptance of `p` and `if p then q` to acceptance of `q` using
compositional clauses, rather than assuming that inference as a primitive
`ValidMP` fact or a bridge whose consequent already is the conclusion.

For a reconstruction using sets of permitted semantic points, pointwise
material implication would make the corresponding containment inference
available. That alone would show only a feature of that selected semantics.
The still-open work is to justify those clauses from the primary passage,
distinguish compatibility from an agent's actual psychological acceptance,
and explain why the encoded incompatibility is logical rather than merely an
undesirable clash of attitudes. Nonempty compatibility states should be
exhibited so the acceptance example does not hold only because no point is
compatible. Distinct moral atoms should be retained in the nontrivial control.

A semantic satisfaction value or a predicate of accepted plans is not by itself
a mind-independent moral fact. Conversely, replacing the label “truth” with
“acceptance” does not establish an expressivist explanation. Any future result
must identify separately (a) the compositional semantic commitments, (b) the
non-cognitive interpretation of the planning state, and (c) substantive moral
realism. A theorem about (a) cannot establish either (b) or the negation of (c)
without further premises. The descriptive-world component must not silently
contain the very moral facts whose necessity is under examination.
