# Hume: induction, habit and practical action

This is the bounded #269 contribution to #247. Its verdict is **consistent but
challenged** for the bounded practical/epistemic reconstruction; the normative dispute remains
**unresolved**. The strong entitlement-denying reconstruction is conditionally
incompatible with added certification/entitlement premises. That is not a
verified refutation of historical Hume. Nothing here assumes Thomism, sufficient
reason, Catholicism or an uncaused-cosmos thesis.

The load-bearing ambiguity is addressed by
`DISAMB-HUME-INDUCTION-REASON-PRACTICE`: psychological propensity, practical
reliance, reasoning-based foundation and epistemic entitlement are different.
The source describes origin and queries foundation. A critic may require a
normative entitlement account, but cannot simply substitute blanket withholding
of entitlement for lack of a reasoning-based origin. `Verdict.unsettled` marks
that difference. Neither withholding nor unsettled entails the event is false.
Metaphysical necessity is a further issue, analyzed by #248/#261 rather than
redefined here. The source's qualified persistence is not metaphysical necessity.

E V.22 offers an instinctive operation that *may* be infallible as an alternative
to slow, fallible reasoning. T I.3.6.13 addresses the broader associative
relations. We neither silently equate those scopes nor turn either into universal
future reliability. E V.5 explicitly stops short of the ultimate cause of custom;
E IV.12–13 similarly limits natural science's ultimate explanations. These
limitations make an explanatory challenge available without making it a
contradiction. E IV.16 even allows the inference as just while asking for its
reasoning; the critic's stronger withholding premise is especially contestable.

## Three attacks and their strongest replies

### Self-application to habit persistence

The strongest candidate asks why the general explanation of future expectations
by habit is warranted if extrapolating any observed conjunction lacks a
noncircular rational foundation. Habit's own persistence is an unobserved
matter of fact. If the account asserts an epistemically entitled habit forecast
while denying *all* such entitlement, its own certification falls under its rule.

`HUME-INDUCTION-SELF-APPLICATION` binds `selfApplication`: from
`CertifiesHabit a`, derive `RejectStrong a`. The proof instantiates the blanket
withholding rule at the habit topic and uses incompatibility of the typed
`entitled` and `withheld` verdicts. It is a bridge-explicit logical transcription,
not a sourced refutation. Both the source's actual commitment to certification
and the critic's strong skeptical reading need defense. The stronger skeptical
statement has independent identity; no claim attributes it to Hume.

The Humean reply is that a customary empirical explanation need not certify
itself from an external, noncircular standpoint. E V.2 explicitly conditions
persistence on unchanged human nature. A present explanation, a conditional
forecast and an unconditional rational guarantee are distinct assertions.
The tests exhibit conditional persistence with effective custom at time zero
and no effective custom at time one, because the same-nature condition fails.
They also construct, for every finite cutoff, habit traces agreeing throughout
the record and differing at the next episode. Those are mathematical
counterhistories; their physical/metaphysical realizability is not proved.
The surviving challenge is explanatory and epistemic self-application, not a
contradiction from E V.2 alone.

### Rational success of prediction and intervention

The strongest candidate combines reliance offered as rational, successful means-end practice,
and the norm that rational reliance requires entitlement. `Assessment.relies`
is scoped to reliance offered as rational, rather than every involuntary
expectation or arbitrary action; that endorsement is an open premise. It presses the
adequacy of habit as an account of rational success, rather than pointing out
that Hume eats bread. E IV.21 already concedes the practical point, while
E V.6 and V.21 explicitly connect custom to means-end adjustment and observed
correspondence.

`HUME-INDUCTION-PRACTICE` binds `practice`: from the explicit open
`RelianceRequiresEntitlement a` and `SomeReliance a`, derive `RejectStrong a`.
An existentially selected reliance topic receives two incompatible verdicts if
blanket withholding is added. The theorem is a logical transcription of this
conditional critique. It does not prove the normative bridge or infer that
metaphysical necessary connection follows from successful practice.

The Humean reply distinguishes instrumental regulation by experience from a
proof of the principle that the unobserved must resemble the observed. Calling
custom natural and useful need not entail denying practical rationality. E V's
note B also permits thought and reflection in science while locating their
ultimate basis in experience. The `limited` model has ordinary reliance and no
reasoning-based foundation, with entitlement unsettled. Even `strong`, which
explicitly withholds entitlement, is consistent with reliance until the critic's
normative bridge is added; its failure to satisfy the bridge is verified.

The successful-trial countermodels give the same observed choices and successes,
but opposite next outcomes of the selected action. They establish that the
finite record alone supplies no deductive guarantee. They do not model
intervention causation, distinguish spurious correlations, or decide whether
success is evidence for a defeasible entitlement; those causal-direction issues
belong to #270. A further abductive argument that structured causal powers best
explain success remains an open explanatory argument, not a proved entailment
or an empirical fact silently built into the tests.

### Selective skepticism across science, cosmology and theology

The strongest candidate requires one invariant epistemic standard when relevant
evidence is genuinely alike. Accepting an ordinary scientific causal inference
and withholding entitlement from an equally supported theological or cosmological
inference would violate that standard. Merely placing them in different subject
areas cannot supply a differentiating feature.

`HUME-INDUCTION-SELECTIVITY` binds `selective`: from `UniformStandard`,
`SameScienceTheology` and `ScienceEntitled`, derive `RejectTheologyWithheld`.
The proof transfers the verdict along the stipulated equal-evidence relation,
then excludes two distinct verdict constructors. This is a logical transcription;
its equal-evidence and standard premises are explicit open frontier obligations.
The verified `BridgeConflicts` parity law quantifies over every pair of `Topic`s,
including science/cosmology and science/theology. The named frontier theorem
instantiates science/theology; its cosmological instance requires its own
independently defended equal-evidence premise. Thus neither cross-domain
comparison gains an evidential premise merely by changing a topic label.

The strongest Humean reply is evidence sensitivity. E V.7 requires an anchor in
present memory or sense; E IV.6–13 stresses experienced conjunctions and the
limits of ultimate explanations even in natural science. A unique cosmic origin
or a proposed divine cause need not have the same evidential basis as repeated
ordinary cases. That reply identifies a possible differentiating feature;
it does not establish that every theological inference lacks evidence. A
`differentiated` assessment accepts science and leaves theology unsettled,
while respecting an equality-sensitive standard. This countermodel establishes
that a uniform standard alone does not force identical verdicts. No source here
asserts a brute cosmological exception or permits one without explanation.

## What the tests verify

`HUME-INDUCTION-TESTS` binds `checks`. It derives explicit counterhistories and
counterassessments and verifies the conditional conflict schemas and shared
answer-map coverage. Natural-number episode indexes, Boolean outcomes, selected
actions and the three typed epistemic verdicts give the tests structure on which
the results can fail. Source interfaces are left open rather than proved by
unconstrained propositional atoms.

The final admissibility countermodel combines absence of the supplied
demonstration with a class admitting only everywhere-matching traces. In that
class a false next outcome is inadmissible. Thus the bare inference from
nondemonstrability to every outcome being admissible is invalid. The class is an
explicit test parameter, not metaphysical accessibility and not a recreated
modal semantics. Nothing in these results establishes that anything can appear
anywhere, at any time; still less is that caricature attributed to Hume.

The three conditional critique results are described as transcriptions in their
result records. Their open premises cannot close a historical frontier by their
mere existence. The direct countermodels in `checks` establish the stated
non-entailments, not the truth of a philosophical worldview. No `refutes` edge
against a sourced Hume statement and no Catholic root-path link is added.
