# From Cogito to acts, bearers and substances

## Three separate philosophical obligations

1. **Occurrence:** an actual act exists (for the Cogito, a thinking act).
2. **Bearer principle:** every actual act has some bearer. With occurrence,
   this entails a bearer, constructively.
3. **Substance principle:** every bearer of an actual act is a substance.
   With both previous premises, this entails an actual substantial bearer.

The third principle is strictly stronger than the second. A finite model
contains an actual act and a bearer but classifies no being as a substance.
Another model has an actual act and an empty bearer relation. These are
logical omission controls, not metaphysical possibility claims.

## Connections to research

- **Descartes:** whether the Cogito warrants an actual thinking subject,
  rather than a first-person grammatical form, remains open.
- **Aristotle/Aquinas:** an account of act as the actuality *of* something
  could independently motivate the bearer principle, but merely defining
  `Act` as bearer-dependent would assume the conclusion.
- **Event ontology:** events or processes may be primitive; the bearerless
  control shows this is not excluded by the weak logical vocabulary.
- **Bundle theories and mereology:** a bearer need not automatically be a
  persisting substance or a mereological unity. Substance requires its own
  independently supported premise.
