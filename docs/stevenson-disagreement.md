# Stevenson: practical disagreement under factual agreement

Issue #185 remains open. The primary witness is Stevenson's *The Emotive
Meaning of Ethical Terms*, Mind 46 (1937), pp. 14–31, pinned in
`STEVENSON-EMOTIVE-MEANING-1937-14-31`. The complete article was materialized

The affirmative answer to Q-MORAL-DISAGREEMENT is practical: a person favours
an object, another disfavours it, and neither is content to leave the other's
interest unchanged (p. 27). This is stronger than merely denying contradictory
beliefs. Empirical reasons can change interests by correcting beliefs about
an object's nature and consequences (p. 28). But factual agreement need not
settle a dispute rooted in differing sympathy or social interests (p. 29).
The latter possibility is encoded, not an assertion that all disputes have
this character or that empirical argument never works.

The new interpretation extends the existing comparison signature and reuses
the earlier model. Its clash relation is now constrained by opposed interests
and mutual redirection. Two agents agree on the nonempty designated factual
domain while clashing over a moral sentence. Factual agreement means matching
beliefs, not omniscience or correctness. Model existence proves consistency
of these encoded commitments only; it establishes neither the historical
theory's truth nor a closure of any Catholic root premise.
