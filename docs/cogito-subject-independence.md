# Cogito: subjecthood is not a consequence of bare occurrence

This source-free investigation compares three interpretations:
1. **Event minimalism:** some thought episode occurs, without a built-in
   bearer or an enduring ego.
2. **First-person grammar:** an occurring token has a first-person marker.
   That syntactic/phenomenological feature does not, without an additional
   bridge, entail a metaphysical bearer.
3. **Bearer ontology:** every occurring thought has a subject. With an actual
   occurrence, the constructive subject-inference theorem produces a bearer.

The two omission models are deliberate: one occurring episode with no bearer;
one first-person-marked occurring episode with no bearer. The multi-bearer
model shows that the bearer principle does not imply uniqueness. None is a
proof that a subjectless actual thought is metaphysically possible.

**Philosophical consequence:** treating a thought as *already someone's act*
builds subjecthood into the premise. That can be a defensible ontology, but
is not an independent derivation from bare thought occurrence. A noncircular
proof must justify the bearer principle by an independent account of acts,
intentionality, unity, or ownership, and confront impersonal-event theories.

No Descartes attribution is made without a materialized primary witness.
Do not confuse an epistemically immediate first-person judgment with an
unconditional theorem of external reality. This result neither establishes
an actual thinking subject nor an enduring soul, persistence, uniqueness,
substance, time, causation or God. No root premise is discharged.
