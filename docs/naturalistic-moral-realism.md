# Objective naturalistic moral realism and Catholic natural law

Issue #249 compares **two substantial primary-source proposals**, not naturalism
as a synonym for emotivism, error theory, relativism or evolutionary debunking.
It extends the question set and V/S/U/C vocabulary of
[the metaethics comparison](../TruthCore/Comparison/Metaethics.lean) and the
availability/content distinction in [AnswerMap](../TruthCore/Metaethics/AnswerMap.lean).
There is no aggregate score, and a sourced answer counts as an answer even when
its defense is open. The same rule applies to Catholic answers.

## Primary witnesses and strongest versions

Both complete primary arguments are byte-pinned `web_snapshot` witnesses and
were materialized before these readings. Copyrighted PDFs and extracted text
are not committed. The physical PDF selectors exclude cover pages; citations
below use printed pagination. Railton is the original journal article;
Boyd is the *Contemporary Materialism* reprint with its own pagination, not
silently the original *Essays on Moral Realism* pages.

* **Railton:** *Moral Realism* (1986), pp.163–207,
  [UNAM primary reading copy](https://www.filosoficas.unam.mx/docs/1110/files/Railton%20Moral%20realism.pdf),
  passage `RAILTON-MORAL-REALISM-FULL`.
  His reforming naturalism identifies non-moral welfare with satisfaction of
  objective interests (§III), then proposes a distinct impartial criterion
  of moral rightness (§IV). Full information concerns what an idealized person
  would want **the actual person** to seek, not what the idealized person wants
  for their own life. The natural reduction basis explains the idealized
  response; that response does not create welfare (pp.175–176). Interpersonal
  comparison and equal consideration are substantive commitments, not a
  deduction from biology, current desire, pleasure or survival alone.
* **Boyd:** *How to be a Moral Realist*, reprint §§1–5, pp.307–352,
  [author-uploaded primary reading copy, mirrored](https://r.jordan.im/download/philosophy/Boyd%20-%20How%20to%20be%20a%20Moral%20Realist.pdf),
  passage `BOYD-HOW-TO-BE-A-MORAL-REALIST-FULL`.
  His Cornell-style proposal defines moral goodness by homeostatically
  clustered human goods and sustaining mechanisms (§4.3), with causal reference
  and empirical moral inquiry (§§3.6–3.8,4.4–4.5). It permits indeterminate
  borderline cases; it does not promise an analytic definition in physics
  vocabulary. Goods include psychological and social needs alongside bodily
  needs. Whether the cluster exists and regulates reference sufficiently is a
  substantive empirical and philosophical hypothesis.

Catholic natural law reuses the existing `CatholicNaturalLaw` bundle and its
consistency result, rather than creating another Catholic model. Its practical
good, ontological actuality and privation must remain distinguished. Likewise,
Railton's welfare, his impartial rightness and Boyd's positive moral goodness
are three roles, not three names for an already identified proposition
(`DISAMB-NATURALISTIC-EVALUATIVE-ROLES`). These selected systems are complete
comparison accounts over these questions **to the extent recorded**, not
exhaustive reconstructions of either author's worldview.

## Shared answer map

**V** means the indicated *scoped* answer has a formal result; **S** a sourced
answer whose defense is open; **U** no answer to that question in the recorded
account; **C** a contradiction of the stated shared datum. In particular,
consistency with moral error answers **can error occur?**, not **has error
actually occurred?** Neither consistency nor a proposed definition establishes
successful objective grounding in reality.

The existing eight metaethical questions are retained, including disagreement
and the evil premise. Mind-independence and epistemology are added for all
three systems. Each cell identifies its answer rather than reporting mere
availability.

## Objections, strongest replies and exact formal consequences

**Identity/reduction.** An objector can ask whether the proposed natural property
really preserves the evaluative role. Railton concedes meaningful open questions
and assesses reforming definitions by normative function and empirical success
(pp.204–205). Boyd distinguishes natural/causal from analytic definition and
allows different conceptions to share reference (§§3.6–3.8,4.5). Neither reply is
“the definition proves itself.” `UNRECOGNIZED-PROPERTY-IDENTITY-POSSIBLE` gives a
nonconstant property literally denoted by distinct names without recognition
of their identity. It defeats only the inference from nonrecognition to
nonidentity. Distinct names do not prove nonsynonymy; nonrecognition does not
prove philosophically significant openness; coextension does not by itself
establish property identity. Moore's full objection and the proposals' semantic
adequacy remain open attacks (`STMT-NATURALIST-IDENTITY-ADEQUACY-OPEN`).

**Normativity.** If all moral obligations must give every relevant rational
agent a reason independently of contingent ends, the two externalist accounts
owe a defense of their rejection of that requirement. Railton separates moral
obligation from individual instrumental advantage and appeals to impartial
justification and improved social arrangements (pp.201–204). Boyd appeals to
sympathy's joint cognitive and motivational function in normal humans while
allowing informed but indifferent agents (§4.7). Neither confuses moral
cognition with actual desire. This is an answered but contested question, not a
contradiction inferred from naturalism. The stronger bridge is recorded as an
open attack, never smuggled into either bundle or into “objective morality.”

The two consistency theorems construct a shared, nonconstant finite
interpretation satisfying each **distinct** bundle, with a true and a false
ascription, cognitive error, and a positive evaluative act without a reason for
an agent. Their frozen Challenges bind the exact types. This establishes
non-entailment of a universal instrumental-reason link **in the encoded
fragments**: the reason predicate is separate and lacks that bridge. It does not
prove a real informed amoralist exists or that every commitment of either
historical system is consistent. The finite welfare and homeostasis predicates
are stipulated witnesses; no biological or social claim follows from them.
Railton's equal-benefit maximizing realization is an example compatible with
the abstract criterion, not an attribution of a complete scalar utilitarian
calculus to him. Boyd's theorem covers determinate positive goodness and never
imposes bivalence on his vague cases.

**Explanatory order and epistemology.** Railton's reduction basis and Boyd's
causal homeostasis purport to explain judgment, not arise merely from current
approval. Both offer fallible learning accounts; neither requires automatic
convergence or takes every culturally entrenched judgment as evidence of truth.
The existence and adequacy of the proposed natural bases remain philosophical
and empirical obligations. The new candidates preserve those obligations
without relabeling the already sourced accounts unexplained. No result proves
Catholicism or naturalism true, refutes the alternative whole worldview, or
closes an ethical root frontier node. The new relations are `attacks`, not
`refutes`.
