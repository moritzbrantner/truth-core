## Distinct Kantian readings

**Cognitive scope.** Categories furnish objective, determining synthetic cognition
only in relation to possible sensible experience. This includes a priori
conditions of experience; it is stronger than Humean observation of repeated
sequences. An inference to something outside experience needs a justification
that the deduction of categories has not supplied. This reading does not assert
that things in themselves are produced by experience or by our minds.

**Objective significance.** The Analytic’s apparently stronger claim is that
categories detached from sensible conditions cannot determine an object at all.
A248/B305 expressly retains a transcendental meaning while denying transcendental
use. Its negative/limiting or problematic role must therefore be retained. This
is not a blanket claim that talk of the intelligible is meaningless. The two
scope claims remain distinct proposition IDs; neither is silently equated with
an ontic denial. Both are constraints in a Kantian account of cognition rather
than universally accepted premises of the comparison.

**Conditionality and necessity.** The fourth antinomy’s thesis and antithesis are
staged conflicts, not two final commitments simultaneously endorsed by Kant.
Their resolution permits empirical conditioning without treating an intelligible
ground as another member of the empirical chain. It also denies that this
compatibility proves the ground exists, or even demonstrates its real possibility.
A state’s being replaced at another time does not establish that the contrary
was possible at the same time. Treating this as simple modal contingency would
change the argument’s subject.

**Ontological dependence.** Kant’s target is a completion of the cosmological
argument: existence of a necessary being, then identification of that being as
*ens realissimum*. The experience of an existent does not determine that
identification. Kant argues that the proposed conceptual identification makes
the most-real concept sufficient for necessary existence, importing the
ontological proof. This is distinct from his scope objection and his objection
to terminating the regress. It is not automatically a criticism of every
possible demonstration from effects.

## Strongest counterreadings and replies

These are reciprocal philosophical challenges, not kernel-certified verdicts.
Aquinas and Leibniz predate Kant: the primary sources state their own positions;
their deployment as replies to Kant is the present inquiry’s reconstruction.

| Question | Independently sourced counterreading | Strong Kantian reply | Remaining obligation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Can an effect warrant a transcendent cause? | Aquinas I.12 [6]–[9]: a *quia* demonstration uses sensible effects without first knowing the divine essence. Origin in sense need not restrict every demonstrated object to sense. | The question is precisely whether a causal category has objective validity beyond possible experience. Calling the step demonstration *quia* supplies no independent license for its transcendental use. | Defend either the restriction on determining cognition or an extra-empirical causal warrant. Neither is assumed universally here. |
| Must contingency reach an unconditioned ground? | Leibniz §§32,36–38: the reason for the contingent order cannot be supplied merely by more contingent members, even infinitely many. This concerns explanation of the series, not its first temporal instant. | An explanatory demand may regulate inquiry without constituting a known object. Mere logical noncontradiction of a proposed complete ground does not establish its real possibility; A609–610/B637–638 contests both the causal extension and reason’s satisfaction at terminating conditions. | Justify a whole-order sufficient-reason principle and its application, or show why the Kantian restriction blocks this particular inferential form. Local causality alone settles neither. |
| Does necessary being mean most real? | Leibniz §§39–41 adds uniqueness, universality and dependence of everything else before inferring unlimited reality. This has more content than bare necessary existence. Aquinas’s *quia* method does not start with an understood divine essence. | Those additions need independently warranted proofs; if their ultimate force is only the concept of the most-real being, the dependence objection returns. “We do not know the essence” does not itself demonstrate the alternative derivation. | Trace each attribute/identity bridge and its sources separately; do not erase this burden by defining “God” as whatever a proof has found. |
| Does the epistemic limit deny an intelligible being? | A realist may affirm such a being while accepting limited human cognition. | Kant himself disallows excluding an intelligible condition merely from empirical dependence, A562–564/B590–592. Compatibility is not speculative knowledge or a proof of real possibility. | This is a point of compatibility, not a refutation of Kant. Practical faith and the complete Kantian moral system are outside this cosmological test’s commitments. |

The non-Thomist comparison is not made artificially independent of ontological
reasoning: Leibniz §45 explicitly offers an a priori proof too. His distinction
between that route and the earlier a posteriori route is his own position; Kant
contests their independence. The research leaves that dispute visible.

The conversion theorem is accepted as a conditional formal consequence: choose
the existing necessary witness, apply the disputed identification to it, equate
any other most-real candidate to it using the explicit uniqueness premise, then
transport its world-indexed existence. Its three premises remain open. In
particular, it does **not** vindicate the further philosophical step from that
consequence to an a priori ontological demonstration. All other results are
concrete model constructions and close only their own scoped model-existence
statements. No result is presented as a source transcription or as progress
closing a Catholic root premise.

## Existing frontier and bidirectional search

The prima via’s `STMT-NO-INFINITE-PER-SE-MOVER-SERIES` is a well-foundedness
premise for an essentially ordered mover relation. Kant’s criticism is recorded
as a warrant challenge to it, not a proof of its negation: the local causal
countermodel deliberately does not satisfy that premise. The third Way’s
necessary-per-se conclusion and the subsequent classical-attribute arguments
have their own act/potency and causal-dependence meanings. Their compositional
results remain intact and cannot be substituted for a Kantian transcendental
license merely by matching the word “necessary.”

Likewise the designated root’s `STMT-GOD-NAMES-THE-FULLY-DESCRIBED-PRINCIPLE`
(and the older reduced `STMT-GOD-NAMES-THE-UNIQUE-MAXIME-ENS`) faces an
identification burden without equating either nominal definition with Kant’s
pure-concept *ens realissimum*. There are two `attacks` relations and no
`refutes`, no `alternative_to` mislabeling epistemic withholding as atheism,
and no new root route.

The #207 pilot supplies the established lesson that a logical result does not
close a philosophical interpretation bridge. No premise-free minimal-truth
result supplies the missing causal warrant here. For #220’s two-sided search,
the legitimate meeting point would require the **same** world/causation sense,
all regress or whole-order premises, and the attribute/identity premises. The
conversion result records all three AND-premises, so reaching its identification
premise alone is not a meeting. Source/scope mismatch stays an open obligation;
no app or graph-search machinery is changed by this inquiry.
