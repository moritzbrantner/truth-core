# Restricted proof denial and explicit theological domains

The owner's example distinguishes refuting “nothing can be proven” from
refuting “nothing theological can be proven.” A search of the existing corpus
found no prior inference making that invalid transfer. The actual nearby cases
are the unencoded q46a2 statement `STMT-WORLD-BEGINNING-NOT-DEMONSTRABLE`, open
`STMT-NATURAL-THEOLOGY-ARGUABLE`, and the selected Hume assessment verdict
`STMT-HUME-INDUCTION-THEOLOGY-WITHHELD`. That verdict is an entitlement
assessment, not an impossibility-of-proof theorem. The existing knowledge
retorsion also already distinguishes universal ignorance from knowing it.
None of these records is silently redefined here.

## One proof standard, with a structural barrier

This analyst batch reuses `Verificationism.ConclusivelyVerifiable`: some
finite, jointly satisfiable observation premises entail the target across
all worlds of the supplied truth-conditional language. It is **one** empirical
standard. Logical consequence from arbitrary premises, independent warrant,
Aquinas's full demonstrative standard, and all possible theological proofs
are different questions. The source q46a2 statement remains unchanged and
unencoded; neither its identity nor a source-strength relation is asserted.

`ObservationBarrier F s` says that every world where `s` holds has an
observation-indistinguishable world where it fails. Its proof could have failed:
a conclusive observation proof would have a satisfiable premise world, and
its observations transfer to the indistinguishable alternative, forcing the
false target there. This contradiction establishes failure of the selected
empirical standard under exactly that structural barrier.

`ScopedObservationBarrier` is the explicit **OPEN** input
`T(t) → ObservationBarrier(target(t))`. Only after it is supplied does the
checked scoped result give `T(t) → not empirically provable(target(t))`.
Calling something theological does not supply that input. Adding defensible
metaphysical/causal premises, restricting admissible histories by a theory, or
using another proof method changes the comparison; no such option is ruled
out by this conditional obstruction.

Global denial strengthens the independently scoped denial. The converse fails
in the finite control: the selected inquiry lacks the empirical proof while
an ordinary observable and a tautology have one. Refuting global denial
therefore does not refute the selected restriction. The strength result is
marked an instantiation transcription; it supplies no philosophical warrant.

## Independent domain definitions

The inquiry table explicitly records subject matter, origins of premises in
the presented argument, and its declared method. These are not definitions of
truth or of every possible alternative proof. Three independent calibration
propositions make the competing senses available:

- by subject: a presentation about the divine;
- by premises: a presentation listing a revelation-origin premise;
- by method: a presentation declaring a revelatory method.

The beginning question is cosmological by subject and theological by the latter
two criteria in the stipulated presentation. A divine hidden-content inquiry
meets the first criterion and not the other two. These are independent
analyst assignments, not historical facts about an author's argument.

`NamedDomainCoverage` is an **OPEN** hypothesis that one named calibration
matches the supplied scope. `RemainingDomain` denies all three on the same
inquiry table. Excluded middle proves structural coverage with that remainder,
not a historical or philosophical census. The selected beginning-only scope
is a genuine different extension: each named class also includes a theological
tautology. That tautology has an empirical proof from empty premises under
every named criterion. An artificially selected class is therefore never
advertised as all theology, and no definition is theological:=unverifiable.

The beginning, divine-hidden and ordinary-hidden presentations have literally
the same target content while their subject/premise/method metadata differ.
Their empirical proof status is the same. The ordinary hidden target has the
same barrier; its failure is not explained by a theology label. The bound
control retains these exact properties, not just incidental model choices.

## Beginning-question and proof-positive controls

The hidden Boolean content is calibrated to the **existing** `PastIsFinite`
criterion in two explicit discrete temporal orders. Ordinary `<` on `Nat` has
an earliest time at now0; reversed `<` on `Nat` has an earlier time before
every time. The observable coordinate is independent of this distinction.
This is an analyst earliest-time profile, not actual cosmological chronology,
metric duration, or an identification of earliest time with a being beginning
with time. Actual evidential applicability and any mapping to the q46a2 source
claim remain **OPEN interpretation bridges**.

The hidden content entails itself when supplied as a premise. That is valid
logical consequence, not an independent proof or warrant for that premise.
The observable content and a theological tautology have conclusive empirical
proofs. These positive controls keep the standard from silently meaning
impossibility of every proof or absence of rational discourse.

## A genuinely different deciding barrier

`BarrierExplainsAll` is the open hypothesis that every empirically unprovable
scoped target has the proposed observation barrier. `AdditionalRelevantBarrier`
supplies a scoped unprovable target without that barrier, opposing its
coverage claim rather than restricted proof denial.

The existing raven-law model provides the second kind: the law is not
conclusively verifiable from finitely many observations, by the imported
checked law-setting theorem. Yet complete observation agreement determines
the law: in the all-positive world, agreement on every `B(n)` forces the law
in any observation-duplicate world. Thus the proposed barrier fails, while
finite proof insufficiency remains. The bound control witnesses the additional
barrier and negates its coverage on a singleton **ordinary** inquiry domain.
It does not thereby refute a separately restricted theological coverage claim;
that would need its own scope/applicability input.

## Self-application and remaining questions

The checked helper `inScopeConstraint` composes the existing scope theorem
through `SelfApplication.Norm` with scope `T`,
false standard and the exact empirical-proof acceptance predicate. Its
existing self-application theorem only applies at an actual in-scope content.
No result places the metathesis or its formulation in `T`, or supplies a
representation bridge showing that an inquiry target expresses that thesis.
An external methodological claim is not made theological merely because it
quantifies over theology. Any self-refutation argument must supply those
missing witnesses under its actual domain criterion and proof standard.

What remains open is the relevance of each theological demarcation, whether
an actual selected question has the structural barrier, which worlds/premises
are admissible, which proof standard is intended, whether a proposed barrier
covers that domain, and the source interpretation bridges. These are explicit
framework/interpretation inputs, not reasons to postpone conditional work.
The original source statement, Hume's selected assessment and the stronger
natural-theology question keep their distinct meanings. No root premise or
actual theological impossibility is established.
