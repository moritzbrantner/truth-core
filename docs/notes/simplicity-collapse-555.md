# Divine simplicity and contingent creation: the rigid-act trilemma (#555)

Outcome: **a certified inconsistent core with surviving branches**, not a verdict on divine
simplicity. Theorems `core`, `rootExposure`, `branchCosts` in
`TruthCore.Results.Modal.SimplicityCollapse.Solution`, over `TruthCore.Modal.SimplicityCollapse`.
They permit the Mathlib three through the #742 import. They establish conditional consequence and
model verdicts. No model is claimed metaphysically possible.

## Investigation brief

- **Thesis under test.** A necessary simple ground can explain contingent things without making
  them necessary. Downstream, this is the main cost of the necessary being route.
- **Strongest case for it.** Necessity of the bearer is not necessity of what it does; an effect
  can be contingent while what the bearer is does not vary.
- **Strongest rivals.** The collapse objection; necessitarian emanation; a brute contingent stop.
- **Reused.** #462 `OutputProfile`, `StableActOutput`, `SameActEverywhere`; #750 `ReasonClosed` and
  rivals; #742 `Marks`, `NetSelects`, `acting`; #755 `Arena`; `IsImmutable`; the IX.6 reading.
- **Decisive fork.** Which member goes, and what explanation is left once it has gone.

## Findings

1. **The core needs less than the objection supposes.** Act necessity, strict efficacy and a
   contingent effect are inconsistent in modal K, with no frame condition and no premise about the
   bearer. A deletion model exists per member.
2. **The core needs more than the objection states.** Both box claims must be read of one token:
   `twoTokens` has one necessary token, another efficacious token and a contingent effect. A
   description denoting something wherever an effect exists does not give a rigid token.
3. **Bearer to act is a condition.** The bearer's necessity gives act necessity only with
   `BeingCarriesAct`. The identity of act and being would give that condition; that simplicity
   gives the identity is not derived here.
4. **What the route itself exposes.** The IX.6 reading and `IsImmutable`, with `ModalCapacity`,
   exclude any respect true at an alternative and false here. A unique actual decree treated as a
   respect then collapses the effect. The fork is: some world without the effect has no decree, or
   `ModalCapacity` fails. Since `oneRespect` has no decree as a respect, there are three exits.
5. **Explanatory cost: the gap moves and does not close.** Every reason-closed founded account of a
   contingent fact has a reason that does not strictly suffice, is not contrast-relevant, and has a
   noncontingent reason for a contingent member.

| Branch | Model | Keeps | Gives up |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nonnecessitating necessary act | `ground`, `inefficaciousAct` | contingency; foundation | strict sufficiency and contrast at the effect |
| Optional act or extrinsic effect | `relocated`, `optionalAct` | strict sufficiency and contrast at the effect; foundation | the same one step back, at the act |
| Brute act | `brute` | contingency; foundation | a reason for the terminus |
| Regress | `chain` | a reason for each member | foundation |
| Necessitarian | `necessitarian`, `necessitarianAct` | every reason necessitates | all contingency here |

Selection does not close the gap: #742's `acting` has marks of choice with a contingent target on
two necessary reasons and no net selection either way, and a one-member base has no such marks. An
enabling condition does not close it: a true condition is itself contingent, and the effect itself
qualifies, so a condition as such explains nothing.

So a closed founded account of contingent creation must deny that each reason necessitates and
that reasons exclude every rival. That price is a standard and not a contradiction: nothing here
shows that the standard is owed. The same price falls on every rival that keeps a
contingent fact, a founded account and closure: the result does not single out classical theism.

## Semantic grounding

**`Necessitates`, `StrictEfficacy`.** Meaning: wherever the first holds, so does the second.
Example: being a triangle and having three sides. Counterexample: a good reason to act and the act.
Distinct from admission as a reason, causation and production. Reflexive and transitive at a
fixed world; not asymmetric, not well-founded. Abstraction: none claimed from experience; it
is a modal test. Rival: a nonmodal account of sufficiency; **OPEN**.

**`ModalCapacity`.** Meaning: what a thing is at an alternative it can be here. Example: wood that
could have had a Hermes cut from it. Counterexample candidate: a merely extrinsic alternative, such
as a statue that could have been admired, with no power to be so. Distinct from the IX.6 `CanBe`,
which it bridges. No transitivity or well-foundedness is claimed. Abstraction from cases of intrinsic
change to divine alternatives is **OPEN** and is what the extrinsic reply denies. Rival: the respects
in question are mere Cambridge properties.

**Decree as respect.** Meaning: a way of being in act, one per world. Examples or counterexamples
in the divine case are unavailable; the ordinary analogue is a person's decision. Distinct from
the effect and from the extrinsic relation of creating. Structural properties are the listed
exclusivity and supply clauses, which are deletable. Generalization from persons is **OPEN**.
