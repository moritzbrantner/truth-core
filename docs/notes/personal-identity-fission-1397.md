## Resurrection interpretation and bridge burden

The primitive Creed record alone underdetermines the question. A selected
Catholic explanatory reading provides evidence for a stronger intended reading:
[Catechism §§988–990](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P2G.HTM)
relates the article to bodily resurrection in addition to the soul's continued
life; [§§997–999](https://usccb.org/sites/default/files/flipbooks/catechism/262/)
describes reunion of souls with their glorified bodies and resurrection with
one's own body, while allowing transformation. This favors a same-subject,
embodied reading over mere production of an unrelated perfect psychological
replica. It does not specify a formal criterion of soul or body identity, demand
retention of every particle, prove resurrection occurs, or demonstrate which
reconstruction captures the Creed's intended metaphysics. This is an explicit
provisional interpretation, not a newly established corpus bridge.

Under a strict numerical-person reading, a numerically distinct replica fails
that identity requirement even if its psychological profile is perfect. Under
an analyst survival-only alternative, a causally adequate successor may satisfy
a stipulated survival requirement without numerical identity; whether this
satisfies Catholic resurrection teaching remains unsupported by the selected
text. A replica with matching memories need not even satisfy causal psychological
continuity. Soul-only survival would also leave the selected bodily condition
unmet. A bodily reading and a strict-person reading need not be rival senses:
they can be jointly required with an explicit person/body bridge.

The conditional incompatibility applies to resurrection only after independently
justifying ALL of: the relevant persons and times; adequate continuation to both
admitted later persons; their numerical distinctness; and the relevant identity
sufficiency criterion. The selector theorem needs the additional claim that the
complete identity-selection facts are symmetric and that a unique deterministic
equivariant rule is required. Neither Christianity nor strict identity entails
such a rule from psychological data alone. No resulting objection to resurrection
has been constructed.

### What is necessary, sufficient, and still unexplained?

For the selected hylomorphic reconstruction, sameness of the numerical rational
soul belongs to the proposed **necessary** identity conditions for resurrection
of this human, alongside the body's relevant numerical identity. Qualitative
sameness of soul-kind or psychological character is insufficient. A positive
candidate **sufficiency bundle** is restoration of the same embodied human
constitutive principles under preserved substantial being. It requires a bridge
from those principles to this complete human being, rather than identifying the
person with a soul or with an ordered pair. These are reconstruction judgments,
not new Lean consequences or independently warranted laws.

The source-backed reply to a bare-token objection is therefore strong: the
individuated soul is intrinsically the form of this embodied human, and its
preserved being plus bodily identity prevents a mere replacement subject. The
reply does not treat soul as an interchangeable pilot. This is a serious positive
framework, not refuted by a model lacking its essential form/matter/being laws.
Its open obligations are to explain and defend those laws without simply
restating the desired identity verdict.

The numerical-soul condition is not an identity oracle. A proposed test must
say how an individually originating form persists, what changes preserve its
individuality, and how attachment to this whole body is determined. An entity
label, a memory match, a continuing concept or a continuously available roster
entry supplies none of these. Independent warrant for immortal individualized
forms is also not supplied by weak non-inherence. The prior causal-identity rival
remains relevant: physically constituted mental occurrences need not imply a
separate immortal bearer, but that rival likewise supplies no resurrection
persistence theorem or completed account of personal unity.

The body condition is not settled either. “Same body” may concern the living
whole, relevant matter, organization or mere material inventory. The restoration
argument needs a defended account of relevant matter and substantial identity
under transformation. It must explain why recomposition restores this whole and
which essential changes would defeat that result. Mere equality of component
inventory would import the contested Theseus Material principle. Conversely,
Theseus's incompatible replacement/inventory/separation bundle says nothing
against a particular human-body account until its bridges and bearers are
matched. Neither a corpse's shape nor a reused atom list is automatically the
same living organism or a complete person.

One soul informing two whole bodies needs separate cases. The per-time
exclusivity restriction forbids two independent whole-body mates at once; it
does not forbid multiple organs of one whole being informed. Across times, an
all-time mate restriction is stronger: per-time exclusivity alone allows a change
of mate. These two analyst laws are not attributed as exhaustive interpretations
of the source. The source's stronger intrinsic-individuation account may already
exclude the sequential case, and cannot fairly be replaced with the weaker law
when evaluating its adequacy. Nor does a rule about body mates alone establish
uniqueness of the complete person without the body/form/person bridge.
