# Law actuality and finite-record identification (#743)

Two individually unique formal laws need not be identifiable from an observed
finite record, even after mathematical presence is represented explicitly.
This is the new conditional discriminator. It establishes neither real concrete
instantiation nor the impossibility of a justified, fallible law belief. The
actuality of this order and independent epistemic access remain separate OPEN
bridges for #735/#613; #467's why-this-actual-history question remains OPEN.

## Argument and exact new consequence

The strongest proposed route starts with a law's consistency, strengthens that
to a unique solution, then suggests that a uniquely determined order explains
which concrete order obtains and licenses its identification by observers.
There are two distinct missing moves: from mathematical structure to its actual
application, and from the observer's evidence to warranted law belief. The
construction tests exact identification at the second move while keeping the
first visible, rather than inventing an opaque actuality or justification flag.

For each n, `law n false` and `law n true` reuse #737's `Profile`, `Follows`
and `Deterministic`. Both initially prescribe true. Every successor output is
fixed by the rule: true through n inclusive, then its Boolean parameter forever.
Each law is deterministic and has exactly one full satisfying trace, `signal`.
These are time-dependent analyst laws; their mathematical uniqueness is derived
pointwise from their initial value and transition equations. They are not mere
opaque propositions saying that a history exists. The false law's signal is
extensionally the old Hume delayed-failure pattern; the new content is not the
existence of that familiar pattern.

`realized n : History Bool Bool Nat` assigns exactly one state-bearer at every
time, the Boolean equal to that history's signal value. `ExistsAt` is presence
at now=0, so `CoherentAtNow` holds. Bearer presence is stipulated in this
mathematical interpretation, not inferred from `Bool` being inhabited and not
certified as actual concrete instantiation. No efficient-cause or explanation
relation is invented; `Causes` is empty. All observations are exact values of
the realized state, including false-value propositions; this avoids selective
retention of positive observations.

For **every arbitrary deterministic estimator** invariant under agreement on
the entire prefix through n, correctness on both candidate law labels would
force false=true. Hence it cannot identify both. This quantifies over all
record-only functions, with no computability, simplicity or persistence-method
restriction. It differs from #737's particular persistence forecast in a single
nondeterministic `openFuture` profile: here BOTH candidate laws are separately
deterministic with unique full solutions. Additional represented population
does not break their recorded observational equivalence. The history fields
are tied to those same signals, rather than unrelated predictive-status flags.

The composition uses exact
`TruthCore.Results.Explanation.HistorySelection.no_selection` and
`characterization`. `readings n w (t,b)` says the observed state equals b;
`window n` admits all t<n+1 and both b values. Both histories agree on that
**whole** vector. At n+1 the present state differs. At either designation the
accessible rival therefore defeats `Determines` and every `EvidenceOnly` /
`Selects` candidate, including arbitrary infinitary recombinations of the finite
record. This is an informational obstruction under the existing entailing
selection standard, not an independent explanatory-adequacy theorem.

The quantifier order matters: for each finite prefix length there is a pair of
laws. It is NOT a fixed pair differing while agreeing at every finite time.
Nor does the result say that each estimator fails on the actual law, that the
rival laws are genuine metaphysical possibilities, that they are equally simple,
or that neither law can be justified defeasibly. The true-law branch is stable
forever. An unexplained law need not yield inaccurate predictions.

## Omission and positive controls

`omissions` removes precisely relevant restrictions. The first unobserved value
at n+1 identifies both labels, but this estimator is not record-only. Admitting
all value propositions determines either full presence history. Retaining only
the true-law candidate lets the constant-true record-only estimator get that
candidate right. The theorem does not independently justify excluding its rival
or assert that the extra observation is already available.

`emptyRealization` removes all world-relative existence and temporal presence,
while preserving the candidate types and law satisfaction predicate. It remains
coherent at now and both laws retain exactly one satisfying mathematical trace.
Thus this formal law predicate contains no population guarantee. This is not a
counterexample to a richer physically interpreted law whose meaning already
includes actual instantiated properties. Such a richer application premise must
be stated and defended, rather than inferred from formal satisfiability alone.
The control does not say that a uniquely solvable law has a satisfying *empty
trace*: the unique mathematical trace is still nonempty as a function; its
represented physical population is what was removed.

The omission certificate directly consumes
`TruthCore.Results.Causality.ChainInstantiation.controls : Controls` (#704).
Its first conjunct compares existing finite and infinite populations with the
same bounded presence/edge data, and its second keeps the same universal
`LocalTransmission` equation with empty versus full presence. We do not reprove
those controls or count them as this issue's discovery. Their distinction
between candidate-domain inhabitance and world-relative existence is retained.
A unique formal solution strengthens consistency but still is not an empirical
or metaphysical instantiation bridge.

Goldman's historical/process account is an especially strong epistemic reply:
fallible justified belief need not satisfy exact uniform identification across
all analyst alternatives. Reliability and available correction conditions need
independent defense; our theorem neither verifies those nor refutes his account.
A local event can remain explicable conditional on an actually applicable law,
mechanism and starting conditions while the ultimate why-question remains open.
