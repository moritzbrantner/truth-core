# Agrippa's regress challenge: what is still owed?

## Question and source

The owner asks why an infinite regress can fail to supply the justification or
explanation for which it was offered. This is an adequacy question. Exhibiting
an infinite graph, a coherent set of local equations, or a fixed point does not
answer it; neither does calling every infinite chain vicious.

Primary witness: Sextus Empiricus, *Outlines of Pyrrhonism* I.164–177, in Mary
Mills Patrick's 1899 translation, Chapter XV, with I.163–179 read as context.
`SEXTUS-PH-I-FIVE-MODES` materializes the whole public-domain Gutenberg17556
volume. The readings below concern the translated primary book, not Patrick's
preceding commentary. Two live fetches produced identical raw and extracted
text hashes. The live URL has no pinned archive fallback; availability and
future transcription drift remain risks detectable by the existing verifier.

The passage presents **five** modes of suspension: disagreement, regress,
relativity, hypothesis and reciprocity (I.164). It does not name Hans Albert,
state a modern three-branch theorem, or establish exclusivity between graph
shapes. “Agrippa/Münchhausen” names the present investigation; no uninspected
Albert passage or particular modern reconstruction is attributed here.

## The strongest source argument

| Stage | Exact source reading | What remains owed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| An offered proof is itself unsettled | I.166: the offered proof needs another, and so on; without a starting point for establishing the claim, suspension follows | Entitlement to use the offered support as establishing this claim; adding another still-unestablished item does not itself answer that demand |
| Stop by concession | I.168, I.173–174: taking something unproved for granted invites a contrary concession, or simply assuming the original target | A principled entitlement distinguishing this starting point from a rival assumption, beyond permission to assume |
| Support the two through one another | I.169: each item needs the other to establish it; neither is taken as establishing the other | A reason why this reciprocal arrangement establishes either side, rather than merely restating their mutual dependence |
| Select a disputed criterion | I.170–177: the proposed sensible/intellectual criterion itself becomes subject to judgment; same-kind appeals recur and cross-kind mutual appeals become reciprocal | An adequate criterion and an account of why its use resolves, rather than relocates, the inquiry |
| State the skeptical outcome | I.165: unresolved adjudication leaves neither acceptance nor rejection | Suspension is not the positive assertion that the object is false, that all knowledge is impossible, or that a causal system cannot exist |

These are prose source readings, not new Lean results:
`STMT-SEXTUS-REGRESS-UNSETTLED-PROOF`,
`STMT-SEXTUS-BARE-HYPOTHESIS-NO-PRIVILEGE`,
`STMT-SEXTUS-RECIPROCAL-SUPPORT-SUSPENSION`,
`STMT-SEXTUS-DISPUTED-CRITERION-DEMAND` and
`STMT-SEXTUS-UNRESOLVED-DISPUTE-SUSPENSION`.

The source's force is not the cardinality of an infinite series. In the
situation described, each new offer remains something **needing** support.
The objection asks what changes its epistemic status. A sequence of renewed
promises to supply that entitlement has not, solely by being a sequence,
specified an entitlement-conferring standard. The skeptical transition from
this situation to suspension remains an epistemic commitment to defend, not
a mathematical theorem supplied by the infinite graph.

In particular, the argument against bare hypotheses supplies more than the
instruction to stop: it asks why the stopping permission favors this premise
rather than its contrary or the disputed conclusion itself. It still does not
show that every proposed noninferential entitlement is merely a concession.
Likewise, the I.170–177 application relies on its sensible/intellectual domain
and continuing disputes; it is not a proved exhaustive classification of every
epistemology. No lack of a chronologically first event is at issue.

## Strong replies and the exact disputed norm

| Account | Affirmative replacement for the unmet demand | Remaining adequacy burden |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Foundationalist | Some perception, rational insight or other noninferential entitlement can warrant a starting claim without another inferential proof | Explain why this entitlement is more than the bare concession challenged in I.168 and I.173–174, and why it warrants this claim |
| Holistic/coherentist | An independently specified whole-system condition can confer warrant rather than requiring a previously warranted member to transmit it | Defend that condition's epistemic sufficiency; a cycle and internal consistency alone do not specify it |
| Infinitist | Appropriately related, nonarbitrary available reasons may enhance warrant without all being occurrently produced or already warranted in the foundationalist manner | Specify availability and the good-reason relation, show that an appropriate chain satisfies them, and defend the resulting adequacy standard; an endless nonrepeating graph alone is insufficient |
| Skeptical suspension | Withhold assent while neither the proposed support nor the offered stopping or holistic criterion establishes the claim | Defend the application of this demanding establishment standard against the three positive replies; do not treat suspension as a positive theorem of universal impossibility |

The positive replies are stated as rival proposals here, not attributed to
Sextus or historically sourced to named modern proponents by this note. The
parallel infinitist investigation supplies actual modern proponent readings;
this table does not stand in for those witnesses. No theory wins merely by
rejecting the others' standards, and the same demand for an adequate standard
applies to the skeptical demand itself.

The decisive open comparison is: **what, if anything, entitles the offered
support to enhance or establish the investigated claim, and why?** Requiring
all justification to be generated from a noninferential foundation would
answer against infinitism only after that norm is independently defended.
Conversely, calling an infinite arrangement “available reasons” does not
supply its promised reason-conferring properties. This note supplies neither
normative sufficiency proof.

## Exact reuse and limits of the existing certificates

- `TruthCore.Logic.Regress.Graph.grounded_eq_lfp`
  (`TruthCore/Logic/Regress.lean`) identifies the least fixed point of the
  one-predecessor transmission operator with its inductively grounded
  interpretation. It does not select the correct semantics of real warrant or
  causal exercise.
- `TruthCore.Results.Logic.RegressSchema.justificationTransfer`
  (`TruthCore/Results/Logic/RegressSchema/Solution.lean`) derives a **necessary**
  finite path to a noninferential foundation from `GeneratedWarrant`, provided
  every inferential rule has a premise. It does not prove that a single
  foundation path suffices, that all inferential edges are well-founded, or
  that every epistemically justified claim has `GeneratedWarrant`.
- `GeneratedWarrant` in `TruthCore/Logic/RegressDomains.lean` is an inductive,
  all-premise account. Its rule-premise predicate has no explicit finite-cardinal
  bound. Do not replace the checked type with a claim about uniformly finite,
  finitely branching proof trees or finite psychological processing.
- `RegressSchema.regressSchemaPackage` binds `CrossDomainBoundary`,
  `EpistemicBridge`, `KnowledgeSelfApplication` and the other controls in the
  existing `REGRESS-SCHEMA-PACKAGE`. The same graph can have stipulated local
  causal activity and no generated foundation warrant. `justified := False`
  is supplied in that witness; the certificate does not independently prove
  real justification absent under every standard.
- `EpistemicBridge` needs an explicit edge-preserving map and epistemic
  well-foundedness. A failed warrant derivation supplies neither. In
  `KnowledgeSelfApplication`, applying a person's own norm requires a positive
  knowledge claim, scope and failure of the norm. Mere skeptical suspension
  does not provide that positive claim.

No new graph or fixed-point proof is needed for this reading. Repeating the
source-free successor model would leave the philosophical adequacy question
exactly where it was. Existing completed verification is reused.

## From epistemic regress to explanation or actual power

A transfer requires a further argument about **the relevant standard**, not
just an isomorphism between predecessor graphs. To establish that a source-free
per-se causal system cannot actually exercise power, explain why real exercise
must satisfy the proposed ground-generating condition. To establish that local
reasons do not sufficiently explain an inventory or whole history, specify the
explanandum and the contrastive or constitutive adequacy requirement. Neither
conclusion follows just from I.166's epistemic procedure. A beginningless
**temporal past** is yet another relation.

See the existing Agrippa/Münchhausen boundary in [schemas](../schemas.md), the
[causal regress investigation](../causal-regress-and-grim-reapers.md), and
`SEXTUS-REGRESS-ADEQUACY-NORM` for the next philosophical obligation.

## Premise comparison and rejected readings

Intended and actual root-route premise change: **none**. All 63 open
root-reaching leaves remain; route costs are unchanged. The new statements are
prose source readings, with no new argument, result, inference or root edge.
They do not establish a new norm, refute infinitism/coherentism, or close the
no-infinite-per-se-series premise. Sourcing a challenge is not resolving it.

Rejected: infinity alone is contradiction; every noninferential belief is
arbitrary; every cycle is already a coherentist justification theory; infinite
available support requires actually completing infinitely many mental acts;
`GeneratedWarrant` is source-verified as all warrant; the five modes are a
checked exclusive trilemma; suspension is global positive denial; or rejecting
an epistemic regress automatically rejects causal activity, sufficient
explanation or an unbounded temporal past. Each would exceed either the cited
passage or the bound existing theorem types.
