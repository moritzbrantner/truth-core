# Applying the demand for reasons to one's own stance (#756)

The objection: "If you reject the PSR, why demand a sufficient reason for it?" The
theorems are `stanceMap` and `bridgeLadder` in
`TruthCore.Results.Explanation.PSRSelfApplication.Solution`, over the vocabulary of
`TruthCore.Explanation.PSRSelfApplication`, which extends the #755 arena and the #397
norm schema. Both are zero-axiom. They establish conditional consequence and the
satisfiability of encoded bundles. They establish no PSR, exclude no brute foundation
unconditionally, and discharge no premise on a root route.

No source claim is added. Koons, Pruss, Della Rocca and their critics (#409) were not
read for this work, so nothing below is attributed to them.

## Conclusion

**The charge succeeds** against a stated rule in the same sense. A stance that says
nothing needs a reason cannot demand one. A stance that says every core commitment
needs a reason, and meets its demands, cannot accept a core commitment with no support.
Both are contradictions in stated commitments, and the second bundle's five members are
each deleted by a named model.

**The charge fails** when it moves from one sense of "reason" to the other. The named
`critic` at world `a` demands reasons for the claim that the PSR holds and for its own
core commitments, and meets them. Its root has epistemic support and no ontic reason.
Rejecting the universal PSR does not remove all reasons: the event there has a reason.
So bare `¬ PSR` plus a demand for evidence is consistent.

**What remains is a charge of asymmetry, and it is feature-relative.** A stance that
demands reasons for the PSR claim and not for its own root is selective relative to a
feature they share, and restricted relative to one they differ on. The same `selective`
stance has both standings, relative to `commitment` and `generalLaw`. No theorem here
says which feature is the right one. A feature test is also cheap: any status can be
made its own feature, so that agreeing on it forces agreeing in the status, and a boundary defined by having a reason holds in every arena and
excludes nothing. A serious boundary has to be stated independently of having a reason
and has to be able to fail, as the event-scoped boundary fails in `scene false`.

**The universal demand binds everyone.** No stance, PSR defender included, has at one
world every accepted claim backed by an accepted claim, well-founded support, and
something accepted. So anyone who accepts something and whose support is well-founded
has an accepted claim not backed by an accepted claim. A circle and an endless chain are
named models of the other exit, dropping well-foundedness. The critic can therefore
reply that a defender with well-founded support also has an unbacked claim. That is an
epistemic stopping point, and it is not an ontic one: in the `critic` model the event has
no support and has a reason, and the root has support and no reason.

**What would be needed to establish the PSR** is a premise that crosses from support
to explanation. A demand honored for the PSR claim is not enough: `defender` honors
it where the claim is false. The tested bridges:

| Bridge | Result | Cost or failure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `TrackingBridge`: supported truths have reasons | follows from the PSR; gives it under `SupportCoverage` | It is the PSR over the supported facts, so it cannot serve as an independent premise for that domain. |
| `EvidenceBridge`: truths used as evidence have reasons | follows from the PSR; forbids using a brute truth as evidence | Not implied by the demand (`premiseRoot`). Gives no PSR: `science` keeps a supported brute root that is never evidence. It constrains only facts used as evidence. |
| `GlobalDefeat`: an accessible brute truth defeats all support | with any support, no accessible brute truth; excludes a stance that accepts and demands a claim and meets its demands, when a brute truth is accessible | Valid, and undefended. The rival says defeat is local. Its core's three members are each deleted. At a self-accessible world, with any support, it cannot hold with the other members of the #755 collapse core. |

So, with a brute truth accessible, a stance that holds `GlobalDefeat`, accepts a claim it
demands and meets its demands is inconsistent, and under `EvidenceBridge` a brute truth
cannot be used as evidence. Neither bridge is established. The surviving
rebuttal is the restricted critic: defeat is local, the root is reached from its
effects and never used as evidence. That reply has a cost: under `EvidenceBridge` the
root cannot be evidence for anything, including a prediction. Whether prediction
can do without it is the separate #737 boundary, which is not imported here.

## The same test for a necessary ground

Necessary existence is not a free label. A necessary truth that necessitates what it
explains cannot explain a contingent truth, so the contingent act needs a
nonnecessitating reason, as in `ground true`. In `ground false` the contingent-fact PSR
holds and the necessary ground has no reason, so that model fails `FactPSR` as the
scene does. Each exempts something and owes an account of the line. `EvidenceBridge`
and `TrackingBridge` as stated bind contingent truths only, so they pass over a necessary
ground, which is exactly the exemption whose ground has to be defended. As #755 notes,
the `ground` signature has no predicate for personal agency, so nothing here separates
a necessary personal ground from a necessary impersonal one.

## The two relations

`Arena.explains w r f` (ontic) is `DEF-PSR-STRENGTH-REASON`. `Inquiry.supports w e p`
(epistemic) is `DEF-EPISTEMIC-SUPPORT`; `DISAMB-REASON-EPISTEMIC-ONTIC` keeps them apart.

- **Ordinary language.** "Why did it boil?" versus "why think it boiled?"
- **Examples and counterexamples.** Smoke supports "there is a fire" without being why
  there is one. A cause nobody has noticed is a reason why, and supports nothing for
  anyone. In `critic`, the event supports the root and the root is the event's reason.
- **Not the same as.** Each other, truth (`defender`), or modal sufficiency.
- **Structural properties.** None is assumed of `supports`. Well-foundedness is a named
  member of the regress core, deleted by a circle and by an endless chain.
- **Generalization.** No move from ordinary inquiry to a metaphysical law is made except
  through a bridge listed as a hypothesis.
- **Competing accounts.** A support structure with an unsupported base, a circle and an
  endless chain each have a model. The restricted critic and the global-defeat defender each
  keep a consistent position under this test. Probabilistic support has no row.
