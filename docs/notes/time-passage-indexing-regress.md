The original challenge combines mutually exclusive A determinations with their
attribution to every event. Its successive-predication reply changes the scope
of those attributions. McTaggart says the temporal qualification presupposes
the A structure whose explanation is demanded, or introduces a further A
series carrying the same problem. His additional explanatory premise is that
moving an unresolved contradiction into the proposed explanation has not
removed it. This is stronger and more specific than simply finding an
unending chain.

His ultimate-concept concession matters: rejecting an explanation as circular
is not, by itself, rejecting its subject as unreal. His conclusion also needs
the original inconsistency and the failure of the proposed repair. No general
claim that every primitive fact is illegitimate follows from these paragraphs.

## Strongest replies and their actual burdens

These rows are **analytical reply profiles**, not new historical attributions
to Prior, contemporary presentists, or contemporary eternalists. A reply's
coherent description is not evidence that actual time has its semantics.

| Profile | Positive account | What it must still defend |

| Tenseless relational reply | Earlier/later relations and variation of properties across temporal positions supply the account; past/present/future can be relational descriptions using a reference position. | Why this accounts for experienced passage/change; whether A-essentiality should be rejected; whether index dependence is explanatory rather than merely notational. |
| Tensed primitive-time reply | Objective temporal passage or tensed truths are basic; successively bearing determinations does not mean bearing them all unqualified at one position. | A coherent positive tensed semantics; why it need not receive an analysis in prior, independently explained A terms; whether McTaggart's incompatibility premise applies. |
| Further hierarchy reply | An explanatory hierarchy can be legitimate if it resolves a demand or supplies independently justified structure. | What each level contributes and why it does not merely repeat the same unresolved demand. Mere formal availability of infinitely many levels is insufficient. |

The first decisive fork concerns what the temporal theory owes an explanation
of: whether it must *derive* A determinations from something prior, may accept
some temporal structure as primitive, or can dispense with A determinations.
That is distinct from the subsequent claim that a proposed explanation actually
reproduces the same incompatibility. A respondent cannot answer the second
claim only by pointing to the first choice, and McTaggart cannot answer the
first choice merely by calling an index a second time-series.

## Transfer to other regress questions

Epistemic reasons, temporal positions, semantic indices, causal predecessors
and explanatory priorities are different relations. This reading supplies a
particular proposed adequacy test: does the explanation remove the precise
obstacle, or reproduce it unchanged? Applying that test to Agrippa's reason
regress, Leibniz's contingent series, or an all-borrowed causal series requires
identifying the relevant obstacle and defending the corresponding norm. The
1908 time argument does not prove a first temporal moment, rule out an
unbounded past, or refute an infinite causal or grounding structure.
