# Two necessary simple candidates: one joint finite control

The live question in #319/#320 is whether the same underived actual witnesses
can be simple and nevertheless numerically distinct. The answer below concerns
an explicit subset of the current formal commitments. It does not establish the
metaphysical possibility of two Gods or settle every sense of simplicity.

## One interpretation, not a mixture of omission witnesses

`TruthCore.Causality.NecessarySimplePlurality` fixes two universally accessible
worlds, four rigid beings and three represented respects (`esse`, `drive`,
`heat`). Every being exists in both worlds. Two roots, `left` and `right`, drive
a common instrument; that instrument, moved in `drive` but in act in `heat`,
moves the effect in `heat`. Thus there is actual motion and an actual per-se
instrumental link. The positive witnesses do not depend on an empty world,
empty carrier, absent effect or nonserial frame.

Component priority and ordinary world-local cause/support both follow strict
rank increase: the roots have rank zero, the instrument one, the effect two.
Both roots therefore have no incoming supporter and no components themselves.
They are, however, components of the instrument and effect. That last fact is
an important omission: this interpretation does **not** satisfy an additional
requirement that the candidates enter no other thing's composition (q.3 a.8,
or the case's stronger altogether-immaterial reading).

### What the primary arguments actually supply

*Compendium* I.15 [69988] contains three arguments. The first divides uses of
“God” into equivocal and univocal, and places the latter plurality within genus
or species; its applicability requires defending that classification of the
candidate domain. Sharing the predicate “unreceived subsistent esse” does not
by itself give the Q11 equation `essenceOf g = deity`. The second argues that a
self-individuating essence cannot be shared by several individuals. This
excludes several bearers **of that essence**, not two different essences each
identical with its own individual. The final argument supplies the two-mode
form-multiplication principle quoted in the earlier analysis. I.12 [69982]
connects genus/difference constitution to potency; I.13 [69984] denies that esse
is a genus. Neither passage independently answers a rival denying that all real
difference is genus/difference constitution or reception.

*De ente* c.3 [69874], in the paragraph beginning with the intelligibility of a
quiddity without knowing its existence, gives a more explicit multiplication
inventory: addition of a differentia; reception in different matter; and one
instance being absolute while another is received (the example is separated
versus nonseparated heat). That third mode matters to the scope of the proposed
fork. It does not distinguish **two unreceived** candidates; it does distinguish
an unreceived candidate from received instances. The following paragraph excludes
differentia-addition from esse tantum because the result would include form
besides esse, and excludes matter-reception because the result would no longer
be subsistent esse. This supplies reasons for excluding the listed modes, but
the inventory's exhaustiveness is still a substantive assertion.

C.4 [69875], opening account of the first way of having essence, explicitly
distinguishes divine esse from the universal concept of being. The latter leaves
addition unspecified; the former excludes its addition. Therefore replacing
“unreceived esse” by the common predicate `Exists`, or by a nature described
without any differentiating information, weakens the source's condition. Its
purity must be defended about the actual candidates, not inferred from our
having omitted differentiating predicates from their representation.

C.4's later account of intellectual substances gives a crucial limit on the
composition shortcut: their genus and differentia are taken from the **whole
quiddity**, whereas the material case supplies differentia from a part of the
quiddity. C.3 nevertheless attributes essence/esse act–potency composition to
these immaterial substances. Hence “different essences can be classified under
a genus” does not alone show two real constituents within each essence. Any
adapter from genus/difference to the existing component relation must specify
what the real constituents are and why they exist. This constrains the
[existing q3a5 composition map](composition-causal-scope.md#four-composition-senses-the-genus-to-cause-gap):
its act/potency and essence/esse routes need their own applicability defense;
this finding concerns simple immaterial quiddities and does not classify divine
absolute esse as a genus-member. The source's claim that
immaterial species differ by grades of act/potency also needs defense against a
rival allowing incomparable or nonqualitative essential differences; a finite
list of shared acts does not settle it.

### Strongest rival and the exact missing defense

The relevant rival is a proposed answer, not a claim attributed here to Scotus
or to any historical author. It posits two necessarily existing, unreceived
subjects, each identical with its whole essence and esse. Their numerical
identities are primitive, or their whole essences differ without either being a
realization of a common genus plus an additional constituent. This is stronger
than attaching different names to qualitatively identical elements. It owes an
account of how those determinations are compatible with esse tantum, and a
sufficient explanation of the plurality if an adequate explanatory principle
is imposed. The present source reading does not discharge that debt; it also
does not establish that the rival is inconsistent.

The following distinctions prevent an apparent defense from begging the question:

- **Logical identity:** `x ≠ y` itself yields the distinguishing predicate
  `fun z => z = x`. Unrestricted identity of indiscernibles with such predicates
  adds no metaphysical ground for identifying the candidates.
- **Qualitative discernibility:** excluding that predicate leaves a substantive
  choice of admissible qualitative respects. A primitive-identity rival denies
  that numerical difference requires a difference within that chosen vocabulary.
- **Specific differentiation:** two whole essences may differ without being
  individuals of one species. The Q11 common-nature premise rules this out when
  accepted, but the suppositum/nature identities alone do not.
- **Real composition:** if an individuating factor is genuinely additional to
  esse as a real constituent, sufficiently strong simplicity excludes it. If it
  is asserted to be identical with the whole subject, calling it a “factor” does
  not prove composition. If it is merely conceptual, it supplies no real
  constituent either. The decisive inference must defend why every proposed
  whole-subject determination limits or receives esse, rather than assume it.

The strongest available conditional defense is consequently: an additional
real determining principle is incompatible with purity, and a receiving subject
is incompatible with unreceivedness. This excludes **those** rival accounts.
It becomes an exhaustive argument only after independently establishing that a
real plurality cannot consist in primitive whole-subject identity or different
whole essences without either kind of determination. The consulted passages
do not derive that universal prohibition from a weaker premise acceptable to
the stated rival. No proof of it has been obtained in this audit.

A tempting circular route is specifically rejected. In c.3 [69874], Aquinas first
concludes that the thing identical with its esse can only be one; **then** he
concludes that every other thing has esse distinct from its nature and receives
it from another. Reusing that latter universal claim to eliminate a second
unreceived essence would presuppose the uniqueness inference under investigation.
The later act/potency account can inform a separately defended participation
argument, but its present ordering is not an independent proof of exhaustiveness.
Likewise absence of a countermodel to stronger metaphysical premises would not
establish those premises.

### Two directions of limitation, and a qualified infinity

Q.7 a.1 co., from *Considerandum est igitur*, distinguishes two directions.
Matter is determined and perfected by receiving form: its prior potency to many
forms terminates in one. Form considered in itself is common to many and its
amplitude is contracted when received in this matter. Infinity on the matter
side signifies incompleteness; infinity on the form side signifies perfection.
Thus a principle saying simply that every limitation implies imperfection, or
that every determining act is a receiving potency, would erase a distinction
on which this argument depends. Matter and form are not two already actual
bodies; no finite rank/component assignment represents this account merely by
using the same word “limitation.”

The body then calls esse most formal and applies the absence of reception to
divine esse. That is a proposed extension of the form-side account, with its
own applicability obligation. “Pure act” as absence of potency in a represented
respect dictionary is not yet the stronger claim that the whole subject is
addition-excluding esse tantum. Even immaterial subsistence is insufficient:
a.2 co. explicitly considers created subsistent forms not received in matter.
They are unlimited **in that qualified respect**, while their esse is received
and contracted to determinate nature. The distinction blocks both identifying
immateriality with unrestricted infinity and treating every whole-form
specificity as matter/form composition.

### The useful implication has an independently open antecedent

Q.7 a.2 co., paragraph beginning *Si autem loquamur de infinito secundum quod
convenit formae*, supplies the precise sequence. A created form subsisting
without matter **has esse and is not its esse**; consequently its esse is
received and contracted to a determinate nature, and it is not infinite
simpliciter. The inferential direction is from the stated nonidentity to
reception/contraction. It does not derive nonidentity merely from having a
determinate nature, nor from the existence of another different whole nature.
Reading it backwards would install the missing bridge rather than defend it.
Its created-form subject must also survive into any attributed formalization;
this is not a universal theorem about every proposed independent being.

The adjacent replies do not remove that antecedent without a new commitment.
The sed contra starts with everything besides God proceeding from God as first
principle. That global causal scope already excludes a second independent
candidate if applied to it; it cannot be used here as an independently proved
consequence of nonreception. Ad 1 says identity of essence and esse is contrary
to being made, because subsistent esse is not created. It argues about what
can be produced, not whether two unproduced subjects can each be their esse.
Using createdness of the disputed second subject to reject it would assume
what the independent plural rival denies. This does not show those source
premises false; it identifies their exact burden in this investigation.

Q.7 a.1 obj.3 directly presses “this and not that” as substantial finitude.
Ad 3 answers that subsistent unreceived esse, precisely as infinite, differs
from other things; the illustrative contrast is subsistent whiteness against
whiteness existing in a subject. Hence numerical or conceptual contrast is not
silently equivalent to the reception the body discusses. The reply offers an
unreceived/received distinction, not a second argument that **two unreceived**
subjects are impossible. Its reference to all others must not be promoted into
an independently defended exclusivity premise.

### Meaning, rivals, and end-to-end application

In ordinary terms the proposed content is a complete constitutive account of
*what a nature is*. A geometric definition illustrates the distinction:
characterizing triangularity is not individuating two particular triangles.

still needs the independent singular-basis and suppositum/nature commitments.
