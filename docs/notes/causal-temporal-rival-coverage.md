### Strongest positive argument and its subarguments

1. **Causal capability before universal causal necessity.** A beginning might
   admit a cause without every actual beginning having one. Ordinary productive
   cases motivate taking causability seriously even where actual causal
   necessity is disputed. An opponent cannot answer this particular argument
   simply by exhibiting an uncaused actual contingent event. The existing
   `POSSIBLE-CAUSE-BRUTE-SURVIVAL` result is important positive scope evidence:
   actual brute contingency can coexist with the exact opportunity premise.
   Thus the modal route is not just universal actual CP/PSR in different words.
2. **Uniformity by relevant features.** The advocate's best additional reason
   is that being the first instance of contingent concrete existence need not
   change the positive intrinsic character of an effect that can be produced.
   If ordinary producibility rests on that character rather than on its place
   in a larger history, the absence of earlier contingent effects should not
   itself remove every possible cause. This is a defeasible analogical
   inference, with a substantive causal-aptitude assumption. It has no supplied
   frequencies or calibrated probability in this corpus.
3. **Separate effect preservation from source availability.** Even granting
   the same intrinsic effect character, an ability to produce it belongs to
   some bearer under enabling conditions. Preserving the effect does not
   preserve a cause while deleting every earlier contingent bearer. Retaining
   an ordinary contingent producer instead gives a contingent member of the
   enlarged history; the resulting event is no longer the first contingent
   reality. Substituting a necessary producer would solve this conflict, but
   its admissibility is precisely the population question. This source-side
   gap remains when one grants that causation need not be deterministic and
   that causes may have contingent effects.
4. **Recombination tests the gap instead of filling it by definition.** A
   principle that rearranges or duplicates already possible contingent
   producers can preserve local production without making one rigid bearer
   present at all relevant worlds. Removing a contingent producer removes
   that producer's exercised ability unless another source is independently
   available. A recombination principle that additionally guarantees an
   existing source outside *all* contingent concreta is stronger in exactly
   the disputed respect. No admissibility class is defined here to require it.
   Nor is the candidate's modal status altered by shrinking accessibility
   after constructing the desired history.
5. **Possible source and causal actuality are separate.** An independently
   warranted real power of a specified bearer could warrant a corresponding
   possible exercise, subject to conditions. But the existence of such a
   bearer with power over the whole first-contingent state is not supplied
   by the ordinary effects or by an absence of detected contradiction.
   Conversely, merely possible descriptions, an abstract law, or a relation
   marked causal in a mathematical model do not supply an actual productive
   bearer. These are precise limitations of this defense, not a denial that
   causal powers can ever ground modal knowledge.

The prehistory argument concerns a temporally prior, existing productive source
of a **first occurrence of any contingent concrete reality**. The current
`Scenario` does not itself encode time, prior existence or this occurrence
analysis: `beginsContingency` is a primitive. If the intended cause is
simultaneous or atemporal, lack of contingent prehistory does not by itself
exclude a contingent member from contributing to the total state. One then
needs a separately warranted exclusion of internal complete production or
another externality argument; numerical distinction from an effect is not
nonmembership in the contingent roster. An exact all-and-only history fact
can include its earlier contingent producer without a contradiction merely
from that producer's earlier existence. Thus this audit does not export the
temporal source argument to A5's history situation or to existential grounding.
It tests a motivating defense of the explicit externality premise, rather
than deriving externality from an unconstrained causal marker.

This analysis leaves a genuine positive reason: causal uniformity can motivate
an initially permissive attitude toward causability. Its defeater is specific:
the relevant cause must survive a change that removes every contingent cause
from the prehistory. The effect's intrinsic qualitative similarity does not
answer this. No additional independent warrant for that source availability
has been identified in the inspected evidence. Mere restatement as 'no special
causal restriction at the first event' would not add it.

### Strongest restriction reply, its price, and the next level

The strongest contingent-natural rival grants actual productive relations,
sound existing causal endpoints, local causal regularity, and even a
well-founded local causal history. It proposes an initial contingent natural
inventory, with subsequent events produced by its members and their powers.
It restricts ordinary causal generalization to histories where such bearers
and conditions are available. It does **not** infer that the initial inventory
is necessary merely because something always occupies the initial role.
Its restriction is motivated by the difference between producing something
within an inventory and producing the first inventory that contains every
contingent producer. This does not prove the restriction metaphysically true,
but it supplies more than an unexplained exception labelled 'first'.
