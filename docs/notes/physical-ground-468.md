# A necessary physical ground against a classical ground (#468, #537)

Outcome: **conditional separators with an omission model on each side**. The theorems are
`core` and `verdicts` in `TruthCore.Results.Alternatives.PhysicalGround`, over
`TruthCore.Alternatives.PhysicalGround`. They establish conditional consequence and model
verdicts. They do not show that either ground exists or is metaphysically possible, and no
root premise changes. A necessary being does not by itself mean God: that is a checked result
below, not a concession in prose.

## Investigation brief

- **Thesis under test.** A necessary impersonal physical reality is a coherent ultimate ground,
  and only further premises separate it from a classical one. Downstream: which steps from a
  necessary ground to the classical God are owed.
- **Strongest case for the rival.** Necessity, underivedness and capacities cost the same on each
  balance sheet (Oppy, below), physics supplies a rich structure, and a composite necessary whole
  needs no outside cause if composition is not itself a dependence.
- **Rivals.** Classical ground; part-free impersonal ground; lawlike structure with no bearer; a
  contingent brute root; two necessary roots.
- **Reused.** `CausalPowers` (#435), `NecessarilyUnderived`, `godIsSimple`, `godHasIntellect`,
  `godHasWill`, Papineau's frame and `allBased` (#419), `ExplanatorySymmetry` (#742),
  `ExplanatoryAdequacy` (#750), `RootMultiplication` (#697).
- **Decisive fork.** Whether composition needs a cause in a necessary being, and how respects are
  classified as had formally.

## The ladder: what separates the grounds

The five tables `noMaximum`, `compositeRoot`, `simpleImpersonal`, `willless` and `classicalRoute` share
`Signature`: the root exists, necessarily, unsupported at every world, with
a capacity at every world, esse-causing a contingent other. The carrier has two beings, so the
common source is over one other being. The rows in parentheses are on the same carrier or scale and
are not asserted to have `Signature`.

| Rung | Model | Premise it drops | Root |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Maximum | `noMaximum` (and `endless`) | a maximum exists | no intellect, all route premises and q.14 a.1 kept |
| Simplicity | `compositeRoot` | `STMT-COMPOSITE-HAS-A-UNITING-CAUSE` | composite, matter-form, maximum, uncaused |
| Immateriality | (deletions of composition kinds, no component, first being) | each premise named | no intellect |
| Intellect | `simpleImpersonal` | `STMT-IMMATERIAL-IS-INTELLECTUAL` | noncomposite, not matter-form, maximum, no intellect |
| Will | `willless` | `STMT-WILL-FOLLOWS-INTELLECT` | intellect, no will |
| None | `classicalRoute` | nothing | intellect and will |

So the strongest composite rival rejects exactly one premise, the uniting cause, and it keeps
necessity, underivedness, capacity and maximality. A part-free impersonal ground rejects exactly
one other. `Core` shows these premises are jointly sufficient given a maximum, so each rung is a
real obligation of the classical route. The first rung matters most: necessity supplies no maximum.
`necessaryPerSeIsMaximeEns` gives identity with any given maximum and not its existence.

**On the other side**, the premises that exclude the classical ground are also explicit. With
Papineau's three premises (the third distributive), composite physical bearers and same-bearer
realizers, a mental occurrence on a noncomposite bearer is inconsistent; one witness per deleted
member shows each is needed. So the classical ground survives by denying a named member, as the
rival survives by denying the uniting premise.

**Containment.** ST I q.4 a.2 alone does not separate. Under `onlyEsse` both survive; `withMental`
keeps only the formally mental ground and `withBodily` only the bodily one, so a classification
chosen for a candidate decides for it. `IndependentlyPure` does not consult the candidate, and its
verdict depends only on which simple beings the table has: a simple mind favours the classical
ground, a simple body the rival, and both exclude both. Whether a simple mind or body exists is
therefore what this route owes.

## Coverage on the same explananda

| Explanandum | What both have | Where they differ, and on what |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Existence of something | `SomethingNecessarily`; the reason is that it had to be so. In `ground` the source has no reason of its own. | Nothing. Self-explanation needs a further account on each side. `rotating` has `□∃` with no necessary being; `law` lacks it. |
| Contingency of laws and constants | A nonnecessitating reason in `ground`. `idle` shows that a necessary being alone gives no reason for a contingent fact. | No attribute is consulted. |
| Unity of what exists | One esse-cause row from the root over the one other being. | Internal unity: `compositeRoot` has parts with no uniting cause, which is either brute or no demand. The classical root has no parts to unite, given the premise. Neither gets numerical uniqueness: the #697 part-free rival survives. |
| Why this world | `state_differs`, `varying`, `oneWay`, `acting` are the same fork for both. | A ground with varying state pays with a contingent state fact, and `core_vary` bars an all-contingent account of it. A ground with one state gets net selection in at most one rival history (`oneWay`). `acting` has the marks of a choice with either flag, so the marks do not reach will without `MarksRequirePerson`, an open bridge. |

The coverage facts consult no attribute row, **by construction**; that parity is therefore relative
to what is encoded and does not refute a richer explanatory demand. A theory holding that intellect
explains selection must supply the bridge or a hyperintensional notion of favouring (#742). The one
explanatory difference the models show is internal unity, and it is conditional on the uniting
premise. A ground whose intrinsic state may vary has an option a ground with no variation lacks. Whether
simplicity excludes that variation is not encoded here; #555 owns that question.

## Oppy (#537)

`OPPY-UNIVERSE-NO-CAUSE-13-22` was materialized and §§VI–VII read. Oppy argues that on general
causal considerations the natural and the non-natural hypothesis give the same reason why there is
something ("it had to be that there is an initial causal state, or ... this particular initial
causal state, or ... this particular part or aspect"), and that necessary existence is attributed on
each balance sheet (pp.19–21, `OPPY-2020-NECESSITY-COMPARATIVE-COST`). The models fit that parity in
the restricted scope and show its limit.

- **NAT-RIGID:** `compositeRoot`. **NAT-QUAL:** `rotating`, `□∃` with no rigid bearer, Oppy's first
  disjunct. **NAT-BRUTE:** `law` and `brute`. **EXT-GROUND:** the classical tables. **Regress:** not
  modelled here; #505.
- **Frame stipulation.** `onlyActual` has one existence table, necessary at the root on a narrow frame
  and not on a wide one. A frame that retains the initial history
  (`OPPY-2020-SHARED-LAWS-INITIAL-HISTORY`) yields necessity relative to that frame, and it cannot
  by itself show that a wider frame is not metaphysical. Oppy's own defense of the frame is not
  claimed here to be absent; the claim records that it is not derived from finite evidence.
- **Not read as Oppy's.** The composite-bearer sense of physical, and any claim that the initial state
  is composite. His "necessity implies independence" step (§VI) is not used: the root is necessary
  and has a contingent effect, which he allows.

No package is eliminated.

## Semantic grounding

**Formal and virtual containment** (`Table`, new). *Meaning:* a fire is hot formally; a thing that
makes heat without being hot has it virtually. *Examples:* a sculptor's hand has a statue's shape
virtually; a heater with a cold casing has heat virtually. *Counterexample to identifying them:*
the two rows are independent in `table`. *Distinct from* causing, which is a third row. *Properties:*
none is assumed. Whether cause must have what it gives formally is the classification, left open.
*Abstraction:* moving from ordinary production to the first cause is exactly what is not assumed.
*Competing accounts:* a causal-powers view has no formal requirement; an emergentist denies it for
mental respects; a classical account requires it for pure perfections. **OPEN:** an independent test
of which respects are pure.

**Esse-causation read as support** (`Route.supports`). An identification for these tables only.
*Distinct from* the general relations kept apart by `DISAMB-EXISTENCE-ESSE-SUPPORT`. No structural
property is assumed. **OPEN:** whether the capacity's response and the esse-cause row are one
relation; the table has them side by side, which is compatible with either.

**Physical bearer** (`PhysicalBearersComposite`). *Example:* a body. *Counterexample to the Papineau
reading:* a part-free field state may be specifiable without mental vocabulary. *Abstraction:* the
inductive base is composed things, and whether fundamental physical entities are composed is
contested. See `DISAMB-PHYSICAL-GROUND`.

## Open steps from a necessary ground to the classical God

1. A maximum for a necessary ground, in shared vocabulary with `NecessaryAt`.
2. A uniting cause for a necessary composite, or a reason the demand lapses there.
3. Positive intellect from the negative antecedent (`AQUINAS-Q14-IMMATERIAL-INTELLECT-BRIDGE`).
4. Will, with q.19 a.1.
5. Uniqueness, with the #697 and q.11 routes.
6. An account of selection reaching will, or the alternative of accepting a stop.

Lawlike structure with no bearer has a separate debt: `law` has no bearer of the source fact and is
not a theory of laws. Sources for the strongest physical monism are still to be read; `compositeRoot`
is an analyst's shape, not attributed to anyone.
