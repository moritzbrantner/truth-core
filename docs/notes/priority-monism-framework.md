# Priority monism: a composite fundamental whole

Bounded investigation for #331/#325, connected to #312/#319/#320. This is a
source-grounded affirmative rival map, not a new formal result or a completed
comparison of all priority-monist arguments. The material cosmos might be basic
and composite while its parts are derivative. Calling it a whole does not by
itself require that parts ground it; calling it basic does not provide a
sufficient intrinsic explanation of its existence.

## Positive account and exact open commitments

Schaffer's domain is actual material objects; deities and spirits are expressly
outside the comparison (§1.1, p.33). He assumes a cosmos with proper parts and
rejects strong composition-as-identity for the debate (pp.34–35). His `Dxy`
means x metaphysically depends on y, separate from `Pxy` (parthood).
Basicness is relative: a concrete x depends on no concrete y (§1.3, p.38).
It therefore does not assert independence from every excluded category or
identity with the repository's ordinary efficient-cause relation.

Well-founded strict priority is openly assumed (§1.2, p.37), rather than proved
against infinitism and coherentism. The tiling argument adds distinct bridges
(§1.3, pp.39–41): completeness requires covering; free recombinability and
constraints on overlapping objects support no overlap; minimal completeness and
whole-to-part duplication support the weaker no-parthood condition actually
used later. These are substantive claims about explanation, modality and
redundancy, not consequences of parthood syntax alone. In particular, rejecting
unrestricted recombinability is a principled rival response to that argument,
not automatically rejection of all fundamentality.

Under that framework the monist's ground of material objects is the composite
cosmos; parts remain real and depend on it (§1.4, pp.42–43). Pluralism assigns
basicness to multiple proper parts. Atomism adds that basics are simples;
pluralism alone does not. Neither thesis fixes every dependence relation among
derivative objects (p.44).

## Shared-question answer map

These are #325's comparison questions, not new logical IDs or a scored ranking.
A sourced answer is distinguished from an independently adequate explanation.

| Question | Priority-monist answer in this reading | Open comparison obligation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Q-COMPOSITION | Cosmos and proper parts exist; composition is not strong identity. | Defend the cosmos/composition assumptions against nihilism and restricted composition. |
| Q-UNITY | The cosmos is one basic material object under tiling. | Basicness and material maximality do not establish substantial unity or intrinsic causal integration. |
| Q-LIFE-SOUL | No answer supplied by this bounded range. | Do not infer organisms, souls or cosmic consciousness from monism. |
| Q-IDENTITY | Whole and proper parts are distinct; real parts remain. | Persistence and replacement criteria are not supplied here. |
| Q-GROUNDING | Material parts derive from a basic material whole; priority need not follow every intermediate parthood edge. | Defend priority, its well-foundedness and explanatory sufficiency. |
| Q-EXPLANATION | The cosmos provides the fundamental material basis, subject to completeness and tiling. | A basis for derivative entities is not yet a noncircular reason for cosmic existence. |
| Q-ULTIMATE | Exactly one basic actual material object in the stipulated arena. | This gives neither uniqueness among all beings nor necessary existence, simplicity or theism. |

The parts-first rival owes a positive dependence account and its own tiling and
foundationalist commitments; it cannot win simply by denying a basic whole.
The simple-independent-ground rival owes the composition-to-actual-dependence
bridge and any nonmaterial scope claims. Neither mereological universalism nor
brutal composition settles the direction of metaphysical priority.
