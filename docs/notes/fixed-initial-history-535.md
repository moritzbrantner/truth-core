## Recursive meaning audit

`Ontic` supplies a frame, `ExistsAt`, and `Causes`; the last two are primitive
relations. `NecessaryAt m a b` is presence at every world accessible from a.
`NecessaryBeing` additionally requires actual presence of the same witness.
It defines neither physicality nor underivedness. The fixed type of candidates
allows literal identity comparisons, but does not warrant that actual entities
instantiate that transworld interpretation.

`TemporalHistory.History` adds primitive `IsAt` and a selected `now`.
`CoherentAtNow` identifies ontic presence with presence at that one time, NOT
with ever existing in a complete history. `BeginsAt` says present at t and absent
at all strictly earlier supplied times; it does not require that t is the first
time, persistence afterwards, or causal priority. `A5HistoryScope` explicitly
separates population onset, member onset, predecessor transition and first
moment. Its contingent-classification predicate is another primitive; it is not
modal contingency by definition. An initial bearer cannot be extracted from a
beginningless history merely by choosing `now`.

`HistoryContrastive.SameHistory` compares every individual's supplied temporal
presence extension. It omits qualitative states, laws and chance outcomes unless
they have been encoded into those extensions. Calling its equivalence classes
“all complete metaphysical possibilities” would strengthen its meaning.
Its `Discriminates` is an entailing contrastive standard, not a neutral definition
of adequate explanation.

`GroundingIdentityProfile` uses a common Bool×B roster. Fixed-tag presence and
world-tag presence preserve actual assignments and source-coordinate matches.
The correspondence is not literal equality. Totality, uniqueness, injectivity,
surjectivity, self-links and exact composition through intermediate worlds
still do not supply `CounterpartNecessity.IdentityBridge`. Local-data invariance
therefore does not choose the numerical interpretation. Conversely, this does
not show that numerical identity is incoherent or that counterpart semantics
is compulsory. The copied grounding/causing edges are primitive interpretations,
not completed analyses of existential dependence.

`NecessaryConcreteUnderivedAt` excludes incoming selected grounds from existing
CONCRETE candidates at every supplied world, even ones not accessible from a.
It does not exclude all nonconcrete explanations. `concrete` is a supplied
classification, not synonymous with natural, physical or composite. The
underived-from-necessity theorem consumes reflexivity, no contingent concrete
support of a necessary target, well-foundedness of the cross-world union on
necessary concreta, and excluded middle. Its existentially selected underived
witness may differ from the original necessary bearer. The union condition is
stronger than worldwise acyclicity. Initial temporal position supplies none of
these grounding policies on its own.

## Positive accounts and their conditional costs

**Fixed-foundation naturalism.** One numerically identical concrete foundation
is present in every admitted complete history; the same dynamics permits
multiple later developments. Its replacement for an external selecting creator
is primitive natural initial reality and nonnecessitating natural dynamics.
This is a positive account of common origin and variation, not merely denial.
The restriction excludes histories with another initial bearer, different laws,
or no foundation. Its price is an independent modal boundary, a transworld
identity account and a stopping point for explanation. Calling the boundary
“metaphysical possibility” supplies no argument for it. These costs are not
contradictions unless a separately warranted inventory, identity or explanatory
principle conflicts with them. A necessary theistic foundation also owes an
account of its modal boundary and contingent effects; formal necessity alone
creates no comparative advantage.

A promising defense appeals to essential identity/origin: preserving this
entity's origin might constrain histories containing it. That does not yet
require its presence in EVERY possibility, forbid possibilities without it,
or preserve the actual laws of nature. The move from conditional identity
constraints to global world-inventory restrictions is the disputed step.
The #1367/#1373 origin/deletion results expose this distinction rather than
settle it. `OriginCompatibleDeletion` is an inventory/recombination commitment;
`OriginDeterminesAccess` links origin compatibility and access. Their combination
entails deletion, while encoded origin tolerance alone leaves deletion open.
Deletion at the actual world differs from deletion at all accessible worlds.
Do not import their stronger-bundle contradiction into the original rival.

### Does a fixed initial history require exactly N simples?

No fixed **number of atoms** follows from necessity of an initial bearer
alone. In a structural model, the same bearer can exist in every world but
have different contingent proper parts. If a **complete initial history**
including constituent identities is held fixed across all possible worlds,
however, its initial roster is constant *inside that modal restriction*;
particle inventories after branching chance outcomes need not be constant.

Oppy's [“Ultimate Naturalistic Causal Explanations”](https://philarchive.org/archive/OPPUNC)
(2013), §§1, 3, supplies a **frugality argument** for shared actual laws and
initial history: objective chance already licenses alternative outcomes, so
additional modal variations require positive reasons. His **Necessary Initial
Part** would have whatever necessary composition that account specifies;
neither its exact count nor the exhaustive metaphysical boundary follows
from the word `necessary`. In
[“O'Connor's Cosmological Argument”](https://philpapers.org/archive/OPPOCA-2.pdf)
(2011), §4, manuscript pp. 5–6, Oppy also considers an initially
**mereologically simple** natural state; subsequent states contain parts
composed of particles and fields. He explicitly marks it speculative rather
than confirmed physics.
His 2020 §VI distinguishes a necessary initial whole from a necessary
part/aspect, so it would misattribute his view to make every constituent
necessarily existent without that extra premise.

The published [Gel](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0034412521000482)–[Schmid](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0034412522000051)–[Gel](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0034412522000713)
exchange asks why a *plurality* of necessary initial simples would number
`N` rather than `N + 1`, and also asks why one first cause rather than more.
Gel treats both an initial state's constituent inventory and a fundamental
layer of necessary simples. Schmid distinguishes one initial **state** from one
**constituent** (including in footnote 9), and Gel's uniqueness-of-pure-act
reply rests on further metaphysical bridges. The exact unresolved test is
whether chance-only alternatives **exhaust** metaphysical possibility and,
if several constituents are necessary, whether their individuation and
number have independent warrant; even a partless one-root model faces a
further uniqueness/explanatory question. This extends the research frontier but
does not add a verified result or close any root premise; see the
[parity case ledger](../principle-parity-challenges.md).

**Qualitative/counterpart foundation.** Histories share an initial structural
role, laws and counterpart matching without containing numerically the same
bearer. This preserves the local explanatory organization in the existing
identity-profile comparison while rejecting the identity bridge. Its price is
explaining why the structural role/laws are invariant and what counterpart
matching means; it gives no rigid de-re necessity for free. Exact composition
of counterpart maps does not settle that debt. A complete description may fix
qualitative structure yet leave haecceity unresolved; adding “numerically this
bearer” to the description would explicitly reintroduce RIGID-INIT.

**Modal recombination/deletion.** Alternatives can omit any given actual
concretum while preserving suitable local/origin constraints. This positively
accounts for contingency by world inventory and access, not by finding a
contradiction in necessary nature. Individual omission need not permit joint
absence; possible empty reality is a stronger claim. Recombination must defend
its scope against essential dependence and necessary entities, rather than
assume that everything concrete is deletable. The origin comparison supplies
conditional examples, not warrant for unrestricted recombination.

**No initial member.** A beginningless natural history has no initial witness
to propagate. Every supplied moment being populated is compatible with no first
moment. An always-present same bearer would be a further hypothesis; changing
bearers need not yield one. This branch needs its own dependence/whole-series
question, not a forced “first member” produced by terminology.

Calling a part-free field “nonphysical” by definition would beg this comparison.

### Oppy's modal readings in the pinned chapter

Read from the materialized witness of passage f8403e5f (Oppy 2020).

- **Strong sentence (§I, p.14).** Every possible world shares laws and
  initial history with the actual world and differs only through the outplaying
  of objective chance; in the same paragraph, chance cannot make "the laws and
  some actual initial history" generate an infinite hotel, so those are
  "outright impossible". This is existing claim c5de6a2a. The text does not
  say whether a shared initial history is the numerically same initial bearer
  (`rigidAccess` below) or the same initial state type (`descAccess`). The
  claim's interpretation already declines the numerical reading; both remain
  analyst interpretations of one sentence.
- **Weaker conditional readings (§VI, pp.19–20).** Oppy argues by cases. If
  causal reality is everywhere contingent, nothing here applies. If it is not,
  and there is no uncaused cause, "any pair of possible worlds share some
  history". If there is one uncaused cause, "either the initial state … is
  necessary, or there is some part or aspect of the initial causal state" that
  is necessary. The explanation is then that it had to be that there is an
  initial causal state (de dicto), or this particular initial state, or this
  particular part or aspect. These are cases in a parity argument, not
  commitments to one modal reading. No new claim record is added for them;
  existing claim c5de6a2a already cites §VI pp.19–21.

Mapping to the encoded profiles: the de dicto disjunct gives only a populated
initial roster at every history. The #535 rotating row already shows this
yields no necessary bearer. "This particular initial state" is `rigidAccess`
if read numerically and `descAccess` if read by state type. "Part or aspect"
needs a necessary proper part, which is not encoded here (see the atom-count
section above).

### Oppy's frugality defense on its strongest terms

The 2020 chapter labels the profile an "austere view about what is possible"
(§I, pp.13–14) without arguing for it there. The frugality argument is in Oppy
2013, §§1, 3 (navigation only; not pinned). Analyst reconstruction:

1. Every modal judgment with evidential standing (outcome counterfactuals,
   dispositions) is accommodated by chance variation from actual laws and
   initial history.
2. A modal theory adding possibilities beyond these needs positive reasons;
   otherwise prefer the theory with fewer commitments that fits the same data.
3. No positive reason for alternative starts or laws is available.
4. So prefer the restricted profile.

This is **not** an exclusion by definition. Premise 2 is a general
theory-choice principle and premise 1 a substantive claim about data coverage.
The restricted profile is excluded by comparative theory choice, defeasibly.

Assessment under the substantive objection standard:

- **Cost accounting (target: premise 2).** Removing possibilities adds
  necessities. In the checked model, rigid M makes bearer 0 necessary where
  E does not. Oppy himself says ontological necessities are always theoretical
  primitives (statement 8aeca775; §VII, p.21). So fewer worlds is not fewer primitive
  commitments unless one adds an accounting rule that counts possibilities, not
  necessities, as the costly posits. That rule is not supplied. This shows a
  gap in premise 2's application, not that the restricted profile is false.
  Against theism, Oppy's own §VI use is parity: both packages posit a
  necessary initial reality or part. Read that way, frugality gives the natural
  foundation no added advantage and no added disadvantage at this node.
- **Practice that treats initial conditions as explanatory targets (premise 3).**
  Cosmological work that treats special initial conditions as needing
  explanation compares alternative initial states (inflation was motivated
  this way; navigation: Guth 1981). That is positive evidence that alternative
  starts are *E-admissible*, and arguably admissible in physical model space.
  It does not supply the M edge, because Oppy can classify those alternatives
  as epistemic or model-relative. This is exactly the inclusion premise above,
  so this consideration does not decide between the profiles.
- **Origin essentialism.** It constrains origin only where the bearer exists
  (#535 `ConditionalOrigin` holds in the rotating omission row). So it neither
  rules out alternative starts for Oppy nor rules them in for his critics.

No other inspected consideration independently rules out alternative starts as
metaphysical possibilities. No substantive objection was identified that shows
the restricted profile inconsistent with an accepted floor item.

### Premise that could affect the necessary-concrete rival

At `STMT-NECESSARY-CONCRETE-AT`, a rigid-reading natural initial bearer *would*
be a witness if it is classified concrete. That requires a bridge from the #535
`History` profile to the `BruteFoundationBoundary` vocabulary, which is not
built here. So this node does not separate the
natural foundation from a theistic one; the separation lies downstream, in the
physical-ground ladder's underived/uniting-cause/intellect bridges. The
precise discriminating premise is **restricted inclusion for bearer-omitting
initial histories**: some E-admissible history lacking the actual initial
bearer is M-accessible. If that premise is warranted, the whole initial state
under the rigid reading is not M-necessary and cannot serve as the rival's
necessary concretum. The rival then retreats to Oppy's "part or aspect" case,
and the same question recurs for histories omitting that part, which need
their own edge. If it is not warranted, the rigid natural foundation survives
at this node.
