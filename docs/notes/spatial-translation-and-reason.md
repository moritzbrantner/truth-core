# Spatial translation, individuation and sufficient reason

The question is whether unchanged relations among bodies settle the identity or
explanation of their spatial placement. This bounded comparison supports the
foundational inquiry without assuming either relationalism or absolute space.
The analyst geometry and the historical arguments have different authorities.

## Consulted evidence and actual argument

`LEIBNIZ-CLARKE-CORRESPONDENCE` pins the Newton Project's normalized transcriptions
of the 1717 English exchange. Both selected ranges, Leibniz's Third Paper §§1–8
and Clarke's Third Reply §§1–8, were materialized and read in full before claims.
The selected Clarke range ends before numbered §9: its combined “7 and 8” is one
HTML paragraph, so HTML paragraph IDs are not treated as philosophical locators.
The modern page's footer/chrome bytes differ from the historical archive copies;
selected text matches exactly. The 2019 Leibniz and 2018 Clarke archive fallbacks
were each fetched twice with matching bytes. No long source text is committed.

- `LEIBNIZ-III-4-RELATIONAL-SPACE`: space is an order of coexistences.
- `LEIBNIZ-III-5-7-SPATIAL-REASON`: given uniform absolute space, a relocation that
  preserves mutual situations allegedly lacks a reason for preferring this
  placement. Leibniz invokes his reason principle and rejects mere will as an
  answer to that contrast. This is his argument, not a proved consequence here.
- `CLARKE-III-2-5-DISTINCT-PLACES`: perfectly alike places can remain distinct;
  unchanged internal order need not mean the same absolute place.
- `CLARKE-III-2-5-8-VOLITIONAL-REASON`: for indifferent or equally good options,
  intrinsic volition may suffice without an externally distinguishing reason.

Thus Clarke does not merely abandon reason. The dispute includes what a
sufficient reason must provide. The source context also prevents identifying
Clarke's space with a separately existing material substance or with God: §3
expressly describes a different account. That context is not needed by the
mathematical certificate and no existence-of-God result is claimed.

## Exact formal certificate and reuse

`SPACE-TRANSLATION-BOUNDARY` concerns labeled configurations `B → ℝ`, with
`shift a x i = x i + a` and selected data `relative x i j = x i - x j`.
Mathlib supplies real additive-group arithmetic and the metric; this does not
introduce a hand-rolled geometry. Existing `HistoryContrastive`,
`SelectedEffectBridge`, the static correction-criterion factorization and the
arrangement-dynamics semiconjugacy results were inspected: they concern
explanatory histories, conditional effects, correctness data or interventions,
and none is a translation-orbit/covariance theorem. Their philosophical meanings
are not identified with spatial placement. The elementary additive cancellation
also used by the parallel measurement-calibration investigation is mathematical
reuse, not an identification of calibration with physical space.

The frozen Challenge advertises one certificate with these clauses:

1. With a supplied body `b : B`, equal signed relative data hold exactly when
   the two labeled configurations differ by one common translation.
2. Every common translation preserves all pair distances. No converse from
   unsigned distances to a translation is advertised: signed differences encode
   more information, including the chosen line's orientation.
3. An inhabited configuration is fixed by a translation exactly when its
   displacement is zero.
4. There is no deterministic function from the selected relative data to an
   absolute configuration that is covariant under **every** common translation.
   The input is unchanged while covariance would move its output, forcing a
   nonzero translation to fix that inhabited output.
5. Subtracting the supplied body's coordinate gives a translation-invariant
   representative with the same relative data. This is a normalization, not a
   claim to discover an actual absolute origin.
6. Two labeled bodies at `0` and `1`, translated to `1` and `2`, have unchanged
   relative data and distance `1`, but unequal absolute coordinate functions.
7. With an empty body type, a covariant selector exists. Inhabitedness is a real
   condition on the obstruction, not an implicit nonvacuity assumption.

The certificate freezes no historical PSR as a hypothesis. Its covariance
condition is an explicitly chosen analyst norm. It also assumes a deterministic
single-output selector; a set-valued or nondeterministic account is a different
problem. The general signature does not include trajectories, acceleration,
rotation, fields, force laws, observability or all physical quantities.

## Positive alternatives and remaining premises

| Shared question | Relational account | Absolute-place/volitional account | What remains open |
|---|---|---|---|
| What individuates placement? | Bodily order of coexistence; translated presentations can represent one relational situation. | Alike absolute places may differ even when the world's internal order agrees. | Whether the selected relational profile captures every relevant fact. |
| What does uniform relocation preserve? | Relations among the bodies in the specified static profile. | Clarke concedes sameness in effect without conceding numerical sameness of place. | Full physical equivalence, especially for time-dependent motion, needs additional laws and structures. |
| Why one placement rather than another? | Leibniz demands a contrastive preference-making reason. | Clarke permits intrinsic volition among indifferent options. | Whether either account independently supplies an adequate reason; PSR alone does not choose between them. |
| What does the symmetry obstruction exclude? | A relative-only deterministic absolute selector satisfying covariance. | A rival may add asymmetric input, deny that covariance norm, or reject the single-output selection model. | An argument that actual explanation or agency owes those precise input/output conditions. |
| Does a coordinate representative decide ontology? | An anchored representative describes a relational orbit. | It does not preclude additional absolute-place facts. | A separate individuation or gauge-equivalence principle. |

Rejected readings are: isometric or translated implies numerically identical;
unsigned distances are the full relational signature; Clarke denies PSR;
relational data determine complete mechanics; the mathematical obstruction
proves either correspondent's metaphysics; and spatial conclusions settle an
unbounded temporal past. No causal edge crosses worlds and no causal relation is
introduced by this profile.
