## Causal meaning and the strongest case

`CausesIn c x r` means effective causal responsibility for x's actuality r. At
esse it concerns x's being, not merely x's coming to be. At another respect it
concerns that actuality in x, not merely c's possessing it. The predicate can
include a primary cause working through a real secondary cause when that
attribution is justified. It does not mean exclusive, sufficient, deterministic,
or temporally earlier cause; no such properties follow from the parameter.

A builder originates a house's arrangement without continuously maintaining
its existence. The continued condition of its materials is a different
explanatory question (q.104). A parent/child example alone therefore cannot
refute transfer from **sustaining** esse. A continuous supply sustaining an
organism or device can be necessary for its operation while the specific act
is selected and produced by an agent or mechanism. This latter example motivates
the rival's distinction, but is not identified with Aquinas's universal
existential cause: ordinary supplies do not give subsistent being to every
creature. A guard removing a threat illustrates mere enablement/indirect
preservation, explicitly distinguished in q.104. A craftsman using an axe
illustrates ordered active contribution (q.105); the craftsman need not create
the axe. These analogies constrain meanings rather than prove metaphysical
universality.

The strongest available positive argument is participation plus concurrence:
all positive actuality is an exercise or determination of being; creaturely
forms and powers participate in being; the primary source gives and sustains
those forms/powers and actively applies secondary agents to operation; thus
primary causal responsibility can include the particular exercise without
excluding the secondary cause. Q.105's different-order explanation directly
answers the objection that a complete primary cause would make secondary
causation superfluous. Primary and secondary responsibility can both be true
in different orders of effective causation.

**OPEN:** dependence of the bearer and its powers is not by itself active
contribution to each exercise. The extra application/concurrence step needs
independent defense. Also open is the identification of a universal esse source
with the source's subsistent/first agent. Inferring either from already named
God's powers and then using it to name that same witness would be circular.
No such premise is used in the new naming proof. The positive argument is
preserved as sourced reasoning and precise missing steps, not falsely certified
as a consequence of existence dependence alone.

## Scope and structural properties

The legacy formula is `∀ c x r, CausesIn c x esse → InAct x r → CausesIn c x r`.
It includes nonuniversal sources and self-effects. The new open statement
`STMT-UNIVERSAL-CAUSE-TRANSFERS-PERFECTIONS` restricts c by the **existing**
`CausesAllInGenus InAct CausesIn esse c` and restricts x to x ≠ c. It retains
the esse antecedent, so the comparison does not require actuality-to-being.
The scope condition says nothing about other perfections, God, maximality,
being uncaused, or `IsFirstBeing PriorSimply`; it is independently
characterized existential coverage, not the desired conclusion renamed.
It is not a complete definition of Aquinas's first/universal efficient cause.

In naming, the retained `MaximumInGenusCausesAllInGenusReading` at esse supplies
this scope condition at the supplied maximum **before** naming. In the reduced
nominal description it is already an explicit condition on the supplied m.
In goodness it follows from the separately retained God-to-maximality and
genus premises; this post-naming use is not fed back into the naming proof.
No maximum, God, or first mover exists merely because these universal
conditional applications are proved.

No transitivity, global asymmetry, well-foundedness, or temporal/modal
necessitation law is added to `CausesIn`. The existing strict causal-ascent
and genus principles are retained as **open**, nonlogical background. Numerical
ranks in the finite models independently justify their strict comparisons;
this justifies the models' order, not real causes' strict ascent. No
same-respect causal cycle is present in either table. Cross-respect dependence
is allowed by the tested vocabulary and is explicitly present in the rival:
0 causes 2's esse, while 2 causes 0's skill. Absence of a distinct incoming
esse cause does not rule out incoming causes in another respect or establish
priority across every causal order. If a stronger notion of first cause excludes
this, that additional condition and its independent applicability are required.
