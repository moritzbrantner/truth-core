# Selecting one outcome over another with a reason true at both (#742)

Outcome: a **certified incompatible core with surviving countermodels**, not a defended
standard. The theorems are `core` and `comparison` in
`TruthCore.Results.Explanation.ExplanatorySymmetry.Solution`, over
`TruthCore.Explanation.ExplanatorySymmetry`, the #755 `Arena`, the #750 rivals and the #736
`SameEvidence`. Each permits the Mathlib three, because the module uses Mathlib preorders and
paths. They establish conditional consequence and model verdicts. They establish no
standard, exclude no position unconditionally, and no model's metaphysical possibility.

## Investigation brief

- **Thesis under test.** A nonnecessitating ground can adequately select one outcome over
  another. Downstream, this is the crux between modal collapse and brute contingency.
- **Strongest case for it.** Ordinary favouring does not necessitate: reasons incline without
  compelling, and weights favour without excluding.
- **Strongest rivals.** Leibnizian renewal of the demand at every contingent detail; a brute
  stop; a noncontrastive reading of explanation; necessitarianism.
- **Reused.** `Arena`, `tokenExpand`, `ReasonClosed`, the #750 rivals and `core_vary`;
  `SameEvidence`; the #755 `ground`. `CollapseCore` is compared and not consumed.
- **Decisive fork.** Whether favouring may vary with the outcome across worlds that agree on the
  whole base.

## Findings

1. **Selection in every history gives what necessitation gives, without saying so.** The core
   has no clause saying a reason necessitates. Yet with a base true at all accessible worlds,
   base-fixed favouring, and net selection and exactly one true field member at each, no field
   member is contingent. This does not define adequate as deterministic: the bar comes from the
   rival's favouring reason being present in the actual history too.
2. **Nonnecessitating selection is available, at the favoured world.** `oneWay` net-selects the
   target at `0`, with its reason true and the target false at `1`. It does not net-select the
   rival at `1`. So a nonnecessitating ground can meet this standard, at a price: some history
   has no net selection.
3. **Opposite pro tanto reasons keep two-way answers and lose net selection.** `acting` has a
   pro tanto reason for each outcome at its own world, and no net selection at `0` or `1`. This
   holds for any frame whose worlds agree on the base.
4. **Favouring settled by the outcome keeps net selection and reports the outcome.** `settled
   ground` has net selection at every world. Under factive favouring, at a selected world, some
   base reason favours `f` over `g` exactly when `f` is true and `g` false. So this favouring is
   modally coextensive with the outcome pair. That is a modal result: a hyperintensional account
   could still claim direction, and nothing here refutes that.
5. **Interposing an act moves the gap to the act.** On any reason path from a fact true at a rival
   to a fact false there, not every reason link preserves truth there. If the act suffices for the
   outcome at that rival, the act is false there while the first reason is true.
6. **Modal difference-making does not help a necessary reason.** A fact true at every accessible
   world enables every fact, and so does a token. Nonvacuous enabling makes the reason contingent,
   and then reason closure, a contingent explanandum and foundedness cannot all stand, by the #750
   core. `brute` has a nonvacuously enabling reason that does not necessitate. This gives the #750
   note its certified difference-making model and also its limit.
7. **Nothing tested separates personal from impersonal.** See below.

## The incompatible core

Fix a frame, `base`, a field `O`, worlds `a`, `v` and a fact `f`. `core` refutes this bundle.
Every deletion is certified, so it is inclusion-minimal, not minimum cardinality.

| Deleted member | Model | Surviving position |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Worlds agree on the base | `varying` | Reasons differ between histories; contingent, and with no reason-closed account of the target |
| `BaseFixed` | `settled ground` | Favouring varies with the outcome |
| `SelectedAt v` | `oneWay` | The rival history has no net selection |
| `SelectedAt a` | `oneWay`, `1` designated | The designated history has no net selection |
| `ExhaustiveAt v` | #755 `ground true` with no favouring | The rival is no outcome fact: whether, not which |
| `ExclusiveAt a` | `overlap` | Outcomes that do not exclude each other |
| `O f` | `ground`, field `oneSource` | The contingent fact is outside the field |
| `f` true at `a` | `necessitarian`, `f` the rival | No rival outcome is true |
| `f` false at `v` | `necessitarian`, `f` the target | The target has no rival |

The core forces rejection of at least one member and chooses none. `varying` also fails `nonCopy`
at no pair: its members are not coextensive with the target, which also holds at `2` by another
member. This list is not a partition of all positions.

## Selection and collapse

`CollapseCore` needs an aggregate, necessitating reasons and more; it is about a BCCF. The second
conjunct of `core` needs no aggregate and no necessitation, but demands selection at each
accessible world over an outcome field. The results are independent, and neither is derived from
the other. They agree on one thing, which is the crux: demanding an answer at every history, from a
base shared by those histories, has a modal price. `ground true` of #755 is the same structure with
no rival-outcome fact, and so the first bundle does not apply to it.

## Personal and impersonal

Earlier results found one structure. This note tries to separate them.

| Candidate separator | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Reasons for each option | `Marks.twoWay`; `acting` has it with any flag |
| Incommensurable reasons | `Marks.plural`; the per-reason structure, which a scalar frame cannot have |
| An act of choosing | `Marks.interposed`; the gap moves to the act (finding 5) |
| Open alternatives | `Marks.open_`; `acting` has it |
| Self-settling favouring | `settled`; coextensive with the outcome, and defined for any arena |
| Being a person | `PersonalStop`; holds exactly when the flag is supplied, so it is circular |

**Ranked against per-reason is a real formal separation**, and it does not sort persons from
setups. A single good order and a single chance weight are alike ranked in form. Competing reasons
and competing tendencies are alike per-reason in form. These readings are the analyst's: no
theorem certifies any of them.

Hence a dilemma, stated in prose and not a theorem. If choice consists in these marks, then
anything with them chooses, including frames read as competing tendencies. If choice is more, the
further content is not among the four marks, and it must say how it bears on which outcome obtains.
Further marks may separate them; this tests four. Self-awareness or normative rightness as such is
unformalized.

## Strongest indeterminist reply and the exact open bridge

The strongest reply rejects net selection at both worlds as the demand. A reason explains an
outcome when it favours it, a true rival reason notwithstanding, and no answer to "why this rather
than that" is owed. `acting` models that, and no theorem refutes it.

**Open bridge.** A constraint on `favours` that excludes `tokenFrame`, keeps some reason true at
both worlds, and is defended independently; together with a decision whether the PSR at stake asks
for net selection or for pro tanto favouring. Nothing here supplies either, for a divine, impersonal
or lawlike ground alike. Under one favouring `ground` and `law` have one net-selection verdict, so
the relevance premise of #750 is not discharged. It is now one named parameter.

## The relation and structural properties

**Favouring.** Ordinary meaning: a consideration counts for one option over another. Examples: a
motive favours the shorter route; a die's weighting favours six over one. Counterexamples: a fair
coin's balance favours neither face; a truth about something unrelated favours nothing.

**Distinctions.** Not `explains`, since a reason may be admitted and favour nothing. Not
necessitation, since `oneWay` favours and does not necessitate. Not probability, since only a
ranking is modelled and no measure. Not causation: `Causes` is untouched. Not evidential support,
`DEF-EPISTEMIC-SUPPORT`, which bears on accepting a fact and not on why it obtains.

**Structure.** No transitivity, asymmetry or well-foundedness is assumed of favouring. `Asymmetric`
and `BaseFixed` are named hypotheses. The case for `BaseFixed`: a die's weighting does not change
because six came up. The case against: for a choice, the opposing account says the choosing is
what makes a reason weigh more. Either is an interpretation, and the core leaves that fork open. A
base can always be enlarged by the fact that varies, which deletes the agreement member instead.

**Generalization.** Nothing generalizes from dice or routes to ultimate explanation. Those examples
motivate the form of the parameter. The theorems are about frames.

## Rejected and open

Rejected: pro tanto favouring as sufficient, since tokens pass; modal enabling as discriminating a
necessary reason, since it is vacuous there; a personal flag as a standard, since it is circular;
net selection reading false base reasons, since it would let an absent reason block selection.

Open: the bridge above; a hyperintensional account of direction for settled favouring; probabilistic
favouring with a measure; a rigid-act treatment of #555, which is an issue-only investigation and is
not formalized here. #751 and #746 were open when this was written and are not consumed. #753's
`PathRelocation` covers temporal paths and was not needed.
