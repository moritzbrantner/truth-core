## Recursive walk and provisional assessment

The following is a finite relevant rival set, not an exhaustive partition;
accounts may coexist. No exclusivity or coverage assumption is asserted.

**Parent: can all-borrowed exercise be positive?** Under the selected bounded,
uniformly contractive, pure-transfer balance profile, no. Outside that profile,
the present evidence leaves the philosophical question underdetermined.
The mathematical result strengthens the conditional obstruction, while providing
no independent warrant for the profile. No conflict with an accepted floor has
been demonstrated for the surviving rivals below.

**Node 1: why should borrowing imply total loss?** The strongest replacement
account is conservative joint transfer: all contributors together preserve
productive magnitude. Two incoming contributions weighted one half yield unit
output from unit inputs. `Controls` checks this scalar row-sum-one identity;
placing it at every binary-tree vertex yields the bounded all-ones solution.
This is not an encoded binary-tree operator theorem. Its price is relinquishing
strict total contraction, not relinquishing individual dependence. Descend to
accounting: if branching duplicates one resource, the account owes a conservation
rule preventing double counting; if contributors supply distinct resources,
summing them can be legitimate. Neither case alone contradicts borrowing.
Next test: formalize a shared resource budget and distinguish duplicated paths
from distinct productive contributions. A physical passive-network theorem
would still need a bridge to metaphysical power.

**Node 2: could every link strictly lose power?** Yes, without a uniform gap.
The checked sequence p_n=(n+1)/(n+2) is positive and bounded by one, and
q_n=1-1/(n+2)^2 lies strictly between zero and one with p_n=q_n p_(n+1).
The positive replacement account is asymptotically lossless transmission,
whose losses shrink upstream. Its price is a depth-dependent profile rather
than a common contraction constant. Descend to cumulative loss: telescoping
finite products give p_n/p_(n+k), with a positive limiting ratio. Thus strict
local loss alone supplies no vanishing tail. The product/limit calculation and
absence of a common bound are not certified by this batch; only the displayed
identities/inequalities are. Next checked node: characterize vanishing cumulative
products, allowing nonuniform factors, rather than demand uniformity by fiat.
No reason has been established to call asymptotically lossless transfer unreal.

Descend to interpretation: each node is finite, so a bound per node does not

## Finite-path extension: deferred exact gap

The proposed iteration is invalid for an arbitrary positive contraction:
monotonicity and a metric bound do not give T(a+b) ≤ T(a)+T(b).
For example, on nonnegative scalars T(x)=max(x-1,0)/2 is monotone,
zero-preserving and contractive, yet T(1+1)=1/2 > T(1)+T(1)=0
(an analyst calculation, not a theorem registered in this batch).
A legitimate extension must specify a positive linear (or suitably monotone,
subadditive) operator, prove the remainder tends to zero, control summability
of the geometric bound, and connect a positive iterate to a finite path in the
*same* nonnegative weighted network, including infinite row sums. Positive
output would imply some finite productive-input path, not well-foundedness of
the entire graph. The next bounded cluster should prove the linear bounded
operator estimate before registering any finite-path conclusion. This batch
claims neither that corollary nor a source-to-being conclusion.
