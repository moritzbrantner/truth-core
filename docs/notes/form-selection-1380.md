## Positive candidate accounts and further tests

A causal-source account fixes reference by a source independently of current
correctness. A noisy sensor can retain that source while reporting falsely;
our faulty witness represents this separation but supplies no causal history.
A remote common cause or a recording's producer may compete with the measured
object as the source. A full account needs an appropriate-path criterion and
an error norm; bare counterfactual dependence does not supply either.

An exemplar account fixes reference by a productive plan: knowledge of how a
form can be produced is compatible with both form capacities. Knowledge of the
actual effect additionally needs selection and fidelity of execution. A failed
execution preserves the plan while making a report of execution false. Defining
all planned content as actually successful would exclude misexecution by fiat.
For divine application one must independently defend guaranteed efficacy and
the act-to-selection bridge; these belong to #555 rather than this new cluster.

A constitutive/external account fixes reference by anchoring a token in its
object/context. The constant-state contextual witness survives this structural
reading; its price is externally individuated content. It need not involve
passive acquisition. But an anchor assignment alone equally applies to an
inscription or replay, so its cognitive adequacy remains unsupported.

These alternatives are analyst proposals, not new numbered senses of cognition
and not exhaustive. ReferenceGround is a shared mathematical interface for their
structural roles, not three independently certified semantic accounts. Distinct
source, plan and anchor maps require separately justified relations before they
can be compared as actual theories. Rejected approach: label three arbitrary
functions causal/exemplar/constitutive and treat their disagreement as a theory
comparison. That would conceal exactly the missing semantic dependencies.

The next substantive test needs an independently specified appropriate causal

application. The current merged vocabulary has none. Extending causal/explanatory
