# Principle parity and justified exceptions

This is a **research category**, not a new truth status, source class, inference
rule or proof of any worldview. It collects arguments of the form: *a proponent
applies a standard to one case, but exempts a comparably situated case*. The
important question is whether the standard really has the same meaning and
scope in both cases, and whether a relevant difference explains the treatment.

The `PP-xx` identifiers are stable **intake labels only**. Canonical
propositions remain the existing independent corpus statements; these rows
do not create arguments, graph edges or verified defeats. Related investigations
should reuse the named notes, issues, definitions and Lean results. The
[category issue #1532](https://github.com/moritzbrantner/truth-core/issues/1532)
coordinates follow-ups without replacing their existing owners.

## Test for a genuine parity challenge

1. **Record the opponent's actual commitments.** Name the same proponent,
   the standard `R`, two cases `x,y`, and the accepted/rejected claims.
   Distinguish `R(x)`, `possibly R(x)`, `can do R(x)`, `exists(x)`,
   and `ought accept R(x)`. Do not silently change quantifier, modality,
   time or causal sense between the cases.
2. **Establish relevant comparability.** State what property makes the cases
   alike for `R`. The claim `R(x) ↔ R(y)` is an additional **uniformity
   bridge**, not a consequence of merely calling both entities `things`.
3. **Ask for a positive discriminator.** A challenger proposes that the
   opponent's exception lacks warrant; a defender can defeat the *parity
   inference* with a concrete, norm-relevant difference. Test that difference
   under the same standard for both cases. Do not accept either an arbitrary
   label (`God`, `universe`, `natural`) or an unspecified possible escape
   as a substantive defense.
4. **Report the exact outcome:** a proved inconsistent bundle under stated
   shared principles; a demonstrated relevant difference; a surviving
   conditional challenge; or an unresolved comparison. Even demonstrated
   inconsistency demands revision of **at least one** commitment: it does
   not automatically establish the favored opposite worldview.

An accusation of *special pleading* is thus a question about the **application
of a standard**, not an extra causal, moral or modal premise. The category
overlaps self-application and companions-in-guilt arguments but is broader:
the two compared cases need not be the argument's own assertion or two kinds
of reasons.

## First worked test: universe versus omnipotent being (PP-01)

Let `U` denote a candidate total universe and `G` an omnipotent being.
Different claims must not be collapsed:

- `◇SelfOrigin(U)`: some coherent possible case has a universe causing its
  own *coming-to-be*.
- `Omnipotent(G) → Can(G, SelfOrigin(G))`: an **additional agent/task bridge**.
- `¬∃G Omnipotent(G)`: denial of actual omnipotent beings, which does **not**
  contradict either counterfactual conditional above.
- `¬◇SelfOrigin(G)`: a **modal impossibility** claim, much stronger than
  nonexistence, whose justification can be compared with `◇SelfOrigin(U)`.

The live target is the **relevant-difference premise**: if the critic grants
actual productive self-origination for `U` but denies even its possibility
for `G`, which difference explains that verdict? The serious proposed
distinctions are a composite versus an agent, reciprocal creation of parts
versus an act borne by the whole, and contingent beginning versus necessary
existence. Smith/Newman's simultaneous-parts universe targets *beginning*,
not merely event recurrence; the positive actual-source principle from
the [origin note](notes/unique-feedback-explanation-311.md) must be tested
against it without begging priority or inventing a temporal requirement.

**Provisional judgment (2026-11-12).** The bare argument “atheists deny an
omnipotent being, so they must deny self-origination” is invalid because
actual nonexistence is not modal impossibility. The *same-causal-standard*
challenge is legitimate and worth investigating, but its bridge from a
universe's internal mutual causation to a hypothetical agent's
self-originating **ability** is OPEN. Nothing here proves the no-self-origin
premise, the existence of God, or inconsistency in atheism as such.

Reversal conditions: a worked account of the same origin relation in both
cases with no norm-relevant discriminator would strengthen the parity
criticism; a justified difference that survives equal application would
defeat that particular parity inference without establishing a general
causal prohibition.

### The strongest new discriminator: reasons for choosing an explanatory stop

Suppose two accounts each put one purportedly necessary source at the base:
`N` is an impersonal natural initial structure and `G` is a necessary personal
source. This pair shares a *formal stopping position*, but three discriminators
are still open and **not equivalent**:

1. **Actuality/necessity:** What warrants the metaphysical necessity of this
   *bearer*, as opposed to mere necessity of there being *some* initial state?
   Oppy's permitted-world restriction is a positive proposal; merely declaring
   the actual initial singularity necessary is not an argument for that
   restriction. A claim that God is necessary likewise needs more than the word
   `God`. Compare the de re/description controls already under #468/#537.
2. **Selection/actual contingent detail:** Does the source genuinely account
   for why this contingent history and its laws obtain, or simply precede it
   in the model? Oppy counts a single natural and a single supernatural cause
   as equal on *general causal explanation*; that comparison leaves this
   stronger explanatory demand untested. A divine choice introduces purpose
   and intention but then owes the connection from purpose to **this** choice;
   a necessarily given source need not necessitate its contingent effects.
3. **Causal category and self-application:** Does a purported ability to
   originate require an already actual power-bearing subject? If yes, apply
   this to the first constituent event of a self-originating loop **and** to an
   act of divine creation; the latter does not purport to bring the already
   existent divine subject into being. If no, identify the actual originating
   relation rather than substituting counterfactual dependence, mutual
   event-causation or a global fixed point for productive existence.

**The label-erasure test.** Oppy's comparative cost counts a natural-only
causal ontology as one kind of cause, versus natural *plus* non-natural as
two kinds (§IV). This is not wrong just because the terms differ. To check
its explanatory relevance, replace the labels with independently specified
properties and causal roles. If *natural* simply means *whatever causes*,
the one-kind advantage risks being verbal and admits any hypothesized creator
as a causal relatum. If it instead means a substantive physical/impersonal
kind, the source needs a positive argument that this kind can be the ultimate
producer of this order; the two-kind accounting must also consider what
further laws or primitive selection facts the models require. Likewise a
term defined as *God* cannot acquire productive powers, necessity or
goodness just from its spelling. This is a proposed discriminator test, not
an attribution that Oppy *does* define naturality as causality alone.

**A concrete logical warning.** `□P` alone does **not** entail `¬Caused(P)`:
consider two admissible worlds, both with `P` and `Q` obtaining, and in both
an irreflexive causal/grounding arrow `Q → P`. One may deny this model by
adopting a definition of causation requiring modal variation in the effect;
that is the *additional bridge* to defend. Oppy's §VI inference from necessary
obtaining to causal independence must not be adopted as a logical axiom just
because it appears in a source. Conversely this counterexample does not
establish any **real** causal relation among necessities.

**Priority investigation, not a finding of defeat.** The strongest direct
target is the claim of *equal explanatory breadth/depth* under Oppy's
theoretical-virtue comparison. Ask specifically whether the same model
answers (a) why anything exists, (b) why these contingent laws/conditions
obtain, and (c) why the source has productive power, with the same standards
in both natural and divine branches. The second target is Wielenberg's
explicitly brute necessary moral supervenience versus a positive,
nondefinitional theistic account of authority. No global naturalist/theistic
refutation or new corpus closure follows from this audit.

## Third worked test: does the source select this contingent world? (PP-03)

**Primary exchange and parent argument.** Timothy O'Connor, [*Is God's
Necessity Necessary?*](https://toconnor.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/OConnor_IsGodsNecessity10.pdf)
(2010), pp. 311–14, directly replies to Graham Oppy's [*The Shape of Causal
Reality*](https://doi.org/10.5840/pc201012227) (2010). Oppy's later
[*O'Connor's Cosmological Argument*](https://philpapers.org/archive/OPPOCA-2.pdf)
(2011), §§6–7, pp. 10–16, gives the detailed positive naturalist rejoinder.
These two authors disagree over **how a necessary source produces contingent
contents**, not merely over whether it is called *natural* or *God*.
The existing [whole-series adequacy investigation](notes/ultimate-adequacy-integration-735.md)
and [#1297](https://github.com/moritzbrantner/truth-core/pull/1297) already
distinguish necessary *bearer* from necessary *content* and sufficient from
nonnecessitating selection; this section tests a new **value-sensitive
comparison** within those open meanings. It certifies no new premise.

### First fix the explanandum

| Distinct question | What a serious explanation must provide | What it cannot obtain merely by a label |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Existential**: Why is there any concrete contingent order? | An actual productive relation or an independently defended reason for denying the demand. | The word `necessary`, `nature`, `agent` or `brute`. |
| **Feature**: Why does the actual order exhibit property `E`, e.g. life-permitting structure? | A mechanism, law, objective propensity or purposive reason that favors `E`, **specified independently** of the observed outcome. | Post-hoc assertion that the source favors exactly what we see. |
| **Token contrast**: Why this exact complete order `W₁` instead of equally permissible `W₂`? | An appropriate selection account under a defended contrast standard, or an explicit account of irreducibly nonnecessitating choice/chance. | Reasons for a *class* of good worlds, when several members remain live. |

The feature question is genuinely intermediate: a nonnecessitating reason can
explain why a **kind** of world is favored without logically necessitating the
unique token. Requiring a complete entailing contrast for every token would
undermine contingent free choice and indeterministic natural production alike;
the existing `HistorySelection`/`ExplanatoryAdequacy` results track this exact
limitation. Conversely, relaxing that standard for one candidate alone is a
real parity problem.

O'Connor's particularly useful challenge concerns the **separate REGRESS
variant** of a **necessary initial segment with intrinsically changing
successive states**, which Oppy surveys but explicitly does not endorse:
if there are genuinely different property clusters `C₁ ... Cₙ`, what makes
precisely that changing series necessary? Calling the *bearer* necessary need
not make each varying state necessary. **But his further claim that necessity
is only contingently joined to each cluster does not follow against Oppy's
explicit restricted-world semantics** when it is extended to that variant:
in those semantics every such initial state may be necessary, and a necessary
earlier state may causally explain a later necessary state. The live objection
is to the **warrant and content of the stronger modal restriction**, not a
derivation of inconsistency from temporal change. **It does not apply as
stated to a model requiring only one necessary initial state followed by
chancy change.** This distinction prevents the critique from begging its
conclusion or attributing the REGRESS view to Oppy as his own.

### Controlled comparative argument: purpose versus propensity

Let `O` be the information that an observer exists; `E` an observed physical
feature **not already guaranteed by `O`**; `G` a reason-guided creative
hypothesis; and `N` a specified impersonal generator. The proposed inference
has the following transparent dependencies:

1. A fully specified `G` supplies purposes and a production account that make
   `P(E | G, O)` high, *on grounds other than merely having observed `E`*.
2. A fully specified `N` supplies physical dynamics and a sampling rule that
   make `P(E | N, O)` lower, *after* accounting for observer selection.
3. With both likelihoods positive and comparable, `P(E | G, O) > P(E | N, O)`
   means `E` favors `G` over `N` as **incremental** evidence given `O`. It does
   not by itself establish a posterior preference, actual design, or the
   existence of an omnipotent/necessary God.

O'Connor (pp. 312–13) explicitly argues for the *epistemic* relevance of
purpose-sensitive fine-tuning even if the necessary source, if real, exists
with metaphysical probability one. That is a valid distinction between the
probability of a hypothesis's *existence under itself* and an **evidence
likelihood**. It does **not** supply the missing comparative likelihoods.
In particular, if `N-fixed` **includes** the actual life-permitting initial
conditions as a necessary axiom, it entails **that initial-condition feature** and assigns its
conditional likelihood one (provided its probability model gives the
conditioning event positive measure); this does not apply automatically to
later stochastic features. The remaining comparison then concerns
the independently warranted **selection of that highly specific natural
theory** and its theoretical cost, not a low likelihood *within* it. A broad
`N` that does not encode these conditions is a different hypothesis and may
have a different likelihood. A similarly tailored `G` that builds the observed
feature into its purposes must pay its own specification cost. Neither side
may win by comparing its detailed post-hoc hypothesis against a deliberately
unspecified version of the other.
The project's [fine-tuning selection study](notes/fine-tuning-selection.md)
has already shown both directions with controlled examples: a selected
natural model can reverse a designer-favoring result, and an indifferent
designer need not predict the evidence. Its observer-conditioned ratio,
continuous-measure invariance and residual-evidence caveats are the formal
authorities here. Re-running an uncalibrated likelihood comparison as a new
proof would merely duplicate those results.

**Oppy's strongest reply (2011, pp. 12–16)** is *not* simply that `G` is a
different label. If the same necessary goals and beliefs leave multiple
intentions possible, a contingent intention still occurs. He asks whether
intentions have an objective-chance distribution; if yes, include that
distribution in the explanatory cost and compare with chance-governed nature.
If no, he offers a substantive **causal-control premise**: a producer must
stand in either counterfactual dependence or an objective probabilistic link
to its effect. Agent causation that admits neither would then lack the
required link. The premise is **disputed rather than a proved dichotomy**:
an agent-causal theorist may offer a third primitive relation, but owes an
independently intelligible account of exercise and control. A critic cannot
make that relation disappear by definition; a defender cannot make it real
by saying `irreducibly agent-causal`.

**Worked contrast.** Suppose `W₁` and `W₂` both permit intelligent life,
but differ in contingent details. A creator's antecedent purpose of allowing
intelligent life can explain why either was eligible, but, without further
content, not why specifically `W₁` was chosen. Likewise a natural generator
whose law favors observer-permitting worlds can explain their prevalence,
but its realized stochastic draw may not explain why `W₁` specifically
occurred rather than `W₂`. A strict token-discrimination standard defeats
both on this setup; a nonnecessitating explanatory standard may admit both.
**Where `G` could really gain ground** is a warranted, independently
specified *value-sensitive discrimination among world-features* that `N`
cannot match under the same evidence and observer reference class. That is
not guaranteed by possessing a purpose, nor impossible for an impersonal
natural process to mimic through a different generative route.

### Named-source stress test: necessity, actuality and a positive motive

Oppy's [*The Universe Does Not Have a Cause*](https://catalogimages.wiley.com/images/db/pdf/9781119028451.excerpt.pdf)
(2020), §I, pp. 13–14 (also retained in the
[pinned passage](../corpus/passages/oppy-universe-no-cause.json)), **explicitly**
treats as possible only worlds with the actual laws and initial history,
varying later objective-chance outcomes.
This is a substantive *restricted modal policy*, not an implication of the
word `natural`. It provides a coherent proposed way to keep initial facts
fixed while explaining later contingent variation through chance. It also
raises a *different*, exact research question: what supports treating a world
with different laws or initial conditions as **metaphysically impossible**,
rather than merely inaccessible to this physical history? His assertion of
the restriction is not, by itself, its independent warrant. In the same
paper's §VI, pp. 19–20, Oppy infers from an item's necessity to its independence
from everything else. That requires a bridge from **modal invariance to lack
of ontic/causal dependence**; the former alone does not logically imply the
latter. A model with two necessary propositions and an asymmetric explanatory
edge is a structural counterexample to the *unbridged* inference, not a
physical example of necessary causation. The existing [#735 assessment](notes/ultimate-adequacy-integration-735.md)
owns the stronger dependence research; do not duplicate its certificate.

By comparison, Aquinas's [*Summa Theologiae* I, q.19, a.3](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/1019.htm)
explicitly distinguishes God's necessary willing of divine goodness from
non-necessary willing of creatures; [I, q.44, a.4](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/1044.htm)
proposes **communication of goodness**, not acquisition of a missing benefit,
as the end of creation. This is a specific *positive purposive account* of
why the first cause could create a good contingent order; it is not merely
the word `personal`. It does not entail a unique creative act, a particular
physical constant, or the truth of divine existence. The linked research
question is whether a necessary perfectly good will can nonnecessitatingly
produce the actual contingent order while retaining the intended divine
attributes. The existing [#1297 choice/contingency controls](https://github.com/moritzbrantner/truth-core/pull/1297)
already formalize part of that problem.

**Provisional symmetry-breaker.** Oppy's restricted modality can make actual
initial conditions guaranteed **inside the stipulated model**, whereas
Aquinas's goodness-based intention provides a reason for a **kind of good
outcome** without necessitating its exact token. These are different
explanatory advantages, not interchangeable probability estimates. Compare
the independent warrant of the modal restriction with the independent
warrant and predictive reach of the motive **under one declared explanandum**.
If both are equally capable of predicting the chosen feature after observer
selection, neither gains that evidential advantage. If a feature remains
unexplained by the natural policy but independently favored by a defensible
divine purpose, that would create a genuine, revisable asymmetry.

### The number of initial simples: the Gel–Schmid–Gel exchange

This is a **conditional numerical-necessity challenge**, not a claim that
Oppy asserts a known number of physically fundamental atoms. Enric F. Gel,
[“How many and why?”](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0034412521000482)
(2022), asks why a *plurality* of necessary natural simples would have exactly
`N` members rather than `N + 1`. He develops **two routes**: how many items
constitute an initial physical state, and how many simples constitute a
necessary foundational layer. He also asks why there is **one rather than
multiple first causes**. Gel's note 18 acknowledges uncertainty about
whether Oppy actually endorses the plurality-of-simples formulation. Joseph C. Schmid,
[“Naturalism, classical theism, and first causes”](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0034412522000051)
(2023), replies that Oppy can propose **one initial whole or singularity**;
Schmid's footnote 9 explicitly recognizes that one initial state might still
have multiple constituents whose number has not been specified. Gel's
[“There can be only one”](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0034412522000713)
(2023) rejoinder defends a unique classical pure act and the challenge wherever
there genuinely are multiple necessary first constituents. Neither response
closes the debate about unique pure actuality or about fundamental composition.

**Oppy's positive rationale matters.** In
[“Ultimate Naturalistic Causal Explanations”](https://philarchive.org/archive/OPPUNC)
(2013), §§1, 3, he argues for *theoretical frugality*: objective chance
supports alternative outcomes without additionally postulating different
laws or initial histories. He posits a **Necessary Initial Part**; if it has a
specified inventory, that inventory is necessary *on this modal proposal*.
In [“Leftow on God and Necessity”](https://www.philosophy-of-religion.eu/menuscript/index.php/ejpr/article/download/159/119/248)
(2014), pp. 6–7, he relates possibility to the outcomes of actual causal
powers and chances but calls this a plausible **metaphysical conjecture**.
In [“O'Connor's Cosmological Argument”](https://philpapers.org/archive/OPPOCA-2.pdf)
(2011), §4, manuscript pp. 5–6, he explicitly entertains a **mereologically
simple natural initial state** with no elementary particles or fields initially;
subsequent states include parts composed of particles and fields. He calls
that scenario speculative, not an established
scientific description of our universe.

| Claim | What follows, and what does not |
| --- | --- |
| `□ Exists(S)` for the initial natural bearer | No fixed part count follows without essential-composition premises. The same necessary bearer can, in a structural comparison, have two proper parts at one world and three at another. This is a logical non-entailment witness, not a physical cosmology. |
| Every possible world shares the **complete** initial history, including constituents | Its initial inventory is fixed **by the modal restriction**. The open question is the independent warrant that the restriction exhausts metaphysical possibility. |
| Only a necessary **part/aspect** of the initial state | Other constituents and later outcomes need not be necessary. Oppy's 2020 §VI explicitly preserves this alternative. |
| A **partless** necessary first state | Gel's *internal plurality* challenge does not apply directly, but **why exactly one such first root?** remains live, as do questions about its necessary powers, properties, existence and capacity for later diversity. |
| `N` distinct necessary first simples | Asking why `N` rather than `N + 1` identifies a genuine explanatory target, but requires an independently warranted modal alternative or numerical explanatory principle to discriminate theories. |

Schmid also offers an **atemporal necessary fundamental wavefunction** as a
possible impersonal one-foundation model, not as confirmed physics. A single
first *state* cannot, just by being called “one”, resolve the number of its
constituents; conversely a genuinely partless root does not hide a plurality
of necessary particles. Gel's pure-act uniqueness argument invokes identity,
simplicity and perfection; Schmid contests the relevant individuation or
Identity-of-Indiscernibles bridge, and Gel defends narrower uniqueness routes.
Our existing [necessary-simple-plurality analysis](notes/necessary-simple-plurality.md)
likewise distinguishes a formal **two-part-free-root** countermodel from
two Gods or two instances of *pure esse*: the relevant common-nature/uniqueness
bridge (Q11) is substantive and must remain explicit.

**Sharper frontier.** Objective chance can generate some alternative histories;
why should it generate **all metaphysically possible histories**? Compare the
proposal with defended recombination/modal alternatives, rather than treating
`N + 1` as possible merely because it can be described. Efird and Stoneham's
[“Methodological Separatism, Modal Pluralism, and Metaphysical Nihilism”](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/102901/1/Efird_Stoneham_Postprint.pdf)
(2013), manuscript pp. 12–15, gives a developed opposing modal framework.
The current result is a conditional explanatory advantage against **unexplained
plural necessary simples**, plus a **uniqueness question** for any stipulated
single first root, not a disproof of Oppy's **single partless**
candidate. Do not infer a fixed number of atoms at later times: objective
chance may produce differing later particle inventories. These are separate
open warrants, not a new Lean proof or root-premise discharge.

### Provisional assessment and next discriminator (2026-11-12)

**Assessment: a potentially substantive asymmetry, still unproved for actual
reality.** Purpose-directed agency has a clearly articulated kind of
feature-level explanatory resource that a **value-insensitive** impersonal
generator lacks. Oppy's mere parity of source counts and general causal depth
does not prove parity on this question; O'Connor's claim that life-permitting
evidence actually favors purpose does not settle the physical or goal-likelihood
premises. The strongest necessary-*natural* generator remains a live rival;
the exact-token problem remains open for both sides. Neither hypothesis is
disqualified by the word `necessary`, `natural`, `contingent` or `free`.

The next **discriminating test** is to select a definite observed feature
`E_res` beyond what observer existence guarantees, and compare (a) an
**independently motivated reason**, specified without tailoring it to the
observed `E_res`, why a creator would prefer that feature over
life-permitting alternatives, with (b) an actual natural generator/selection
model's predictions for `E_res`. The alternative is a philosophical defense
of Oppy's causal-control dichotomy or of a substantive third agent-causal
relation. A demonstrated higher conditional likelihood or an independently
defended explanatory-role asymmetry strengthens the theistic preference;
observer screening, an indifferent/contrary purpose, or a comparably
value-sensitive natural mechanism weakens or reverses it. Keep the disputed
selection rules and source-exercise bridge OPEN; no additional root premise
is discharged, no corpus identity or prior judgment is silently changed.

**Do not combine incompatible rescue moves.** Oppy's 2020 account explicitly
rejects self-causes and causal circles; Newman argues that some causal loops
are directly self-caused. They are individually assessable alternatives, **not**
two premises a single naturalist can invoke together without revision. In a
debate, preserve each opponent's chosen causal theory, modal accessibility,
source type and explanatory criterion across the entire branch. Switching
theories to meet successive challenges is an *alternative-model search*;
the union of its individually successful replies is not one demonstrated
coherent and adequate explanation. The same discipline applies to shifting
among different theistic theories of causation, freedom and divine necessity.

## Fourth worked test: multiverse plentitude and modal accountability (PP-07)

**Question.** A rival appeals to *all possible universes* to explain why some
universe exhibits a surprising feature. Does that commit the rival to God's
actual existence if a necessarily existing God is possible? Only after fixing
the meanings of *universe*, *possible*, *actual*, and *necessary*.

The existing formal authority is
[`NecessaryIfExistent`](../TruthCore/Modal/NecessaryIfExistent.lean), its
[`Controls`](../TruthCore/Results/Modal/NecessaryIfExistent/Solution.lean),
and [`NecessaryIfConnectivity`](../TruthCore/Modal/NecessaryIfConnectivity.lean)
with its [verified correspondence](../TruthCore/Results/Modal/NecessaryIfConnectivity/Solution.lean).
For an arbitrary proposition `G` expressing divine existence at a standpoint
`w`, the latter uses `NecessaryIf F G w` = `□(G → □G)` and proves that
`◇G → □G` holds for **all** `G` exactly when the successors of `w` satisfy
the specified masked-path connectivity condition. It proves stronger
sufficient conditions (Euclidean access; reflexive + symmetric access) and
explicitly does **not** assert that S5 is the weakest logic. The S4 omission
model in `NecessaryIfExistent.Controls` already shows the claimed conclusion
can fail even with `□(G → □G)` and `◇G`.

### An even smaller, local actuality bridge

Let `wRv` mean that world `v` is accessible from our world `w`. Suppose:

1. `wRv` and `G(v)` (a God-world is possible from here).
2. `G(v) → □G(v)` (at that world God would exist necessarily), or instead
   the existing rooted conditional `□(G → □G)` at `w`.
3. **`vRw`** (the God-world can access our world).

Then `G(w)` follows immediately by applying `□G(v)` to `vRw`. This does not
need transitivity, a universal accessibility relation, or S5. Global symmetry
(the B frame condition) supplies the back edge; one *locally warranted* back
edge is weaker still. Reflexivity + symmetry (T+B) additionally supplies the
existing root-level necessity propagation. The target is **actual existence**,
not yet necessity across disconnected components or the identity of an
individual in different worlds. When `G` describes a God-like individual, the
individual/existence-domain and essence assumptions must remain explicit; the
existing [`GOEDEL-1970-POSSIBLE-GOD-LIKE-NECESSARILY-EXISTS`](../TruthCore/Results/Isabelle/NotesOnGoedel/PossibleGodLikeNecessarilyExists/Solution.lean)
uses positivity axioms, essence and necessary-existence definitions, and
symmetry; it must not be quoted as
proving God's metaphysical possibility.

**Exact S4 survivor.** Take `W={w,v}`, accessibility
`{(w,w),(w,v),(v,v)}`, `G(w)=false`, `G(v)=true`. The relation is reflexive
and transitive (S4), with `wRv`, `G(v)` and `□G(v)`, but `¬G(w)` and `¬vRw`.
It is even connected as an **undirected** graph. Thus the argument requires
*return access* or stronger connectivity, not mere undirected connectedness.
This is a real countermodel to the overstrong S4 inference, not an objection
to the repaired local argument. **S4 + global B is S5 at frame level**;
claiming it as a globally weaker system would be misleading. B alone or a
single return edge is the useful weaker route.

### Three different plenitude claims, not one opponent

| Plenitude reading | What it commits to | Relevant comparison and open bridge |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Metaphysical maximality:** every metaphysically possible world exists as a concrete reality. | A genuine global modal-ontology claim. In David Lewis's [*On the Plurality of Worlds*](https://www.wiley.com/en-us/on-the-plurality-of-worlds-p-9780631224969), ch. 1, actuality is indexical: every world is actual *at itself*, **not** every world actual *here*. | If a **necessarily** existing God is metaphysically possible under the same modality and an appropriate return-access/necessity principle holds, actual existence follows. The proponent can reject divine possibility or that bridge, but owes a positive account of the exclusion where they assert unrestricted plenitude. Do not infer `G(w)` solely from the claim that a *contingent* God occurs in some other real world. |
| **Physical plenitude:** every reachable vacuum or universe allowed by a generator occurs. | A claim about the possibilities of that *specific physical dynamics*, not all metaphysical possibilities. Bousso–Polchinski's [quantized flux discretuum](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0004134) illustrates constrained allowed vacuum values rather than every freely combined set of constants. | Test derivation of the laws, correlated parameters, dynamical reachability and populated vacua. The exclusion of supernatural beings is **not** contradictory to a physical-only generative claim unless the proponent also asserts metaphysical completeness. It may still leave an explanatory stop. |
| **Branching/ensemble:** many decohered outcomes, inflationary bubbles or universes are generated. | Multiple physical outcomes, not automatically different fundamental laws or all modal alternatives. An [eternal-inflation/landscape analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11189337/) notes that inflation alone need not vary life-relevant parameters. | Demand the actual parameter-variation mechanism, observer/measure rule and discrimination beyond anthropic viability. Do not merge Everett-style branching, inflationary bubbles, landscape vacua and Lewisian worlds into one theory. |

**The dialectical result.** An atheist *as such* does not have to endorse
possible-world realism, S5, or a complete metaphysical theory before being
permitted to disagree. But a particular rival who **uses** claims such as
`all possible universes obtain`, `God is impossible`, `the actual laws are
necessary`, or `a different causal origin was impossible` incurs a local
**modal-accountability obligation**: specify the relevant modality,
accessibility/domain, and a substantive warrant for the exclusions and
identities that the inference employs. This applies symmetrically to theists
who assert `◇G` without defending its compossibility with the proposed
attributes. A requirement to **articulate** a live relevant difference is
not a theorem that every naturalist owes a total modal ontology or that
`◇G` is already established.

**Bayesian connection, with a separate evidential role.** Let `D` be a
specified purposive-design hypothesis, `N` a specified physical ensemble,
`O` observer-existence/background information and `E` a measured residual
feature not guaranteed by `O`. For positive denominators, the relevant
posterior comparison is

`P(D | E,O) / P(N | E,O) = [P(D | O) / P(N | O)] × [P(E | O,D) / P(E | O,N)]`.

The exact [fine-tuning selection controls](notes/fine-tuning-selection.md)
already show both how observer filtering can reverse a Bayes-factor direction
and how an underjustified design-preference distribution can do so. A modal
consistency failure may **eliminate a particular joint hypothesis** before
comparison, but the mere possibility of God provides no numerical design
likelihood, no equal 50:50 prior, and no empirical conclusion. Conversely, a
physical multiverse must supply an actual generating distribution and a
record-selection measure to make its advertised predictive comparison. The
modal and probabilistic tests therefore accumulate *different conditional
constraints* on one candidate theory; counting them as independent numerical
evidence would double count unsupported bridges.

**Provisional judgment (2026-11-12).** The local return-edge and the S4
counterexample are sound, already supported by the project's frame work.
This gives an important *conditional* challenge to unrestricted modal
plentitude, not a general defeat of physical multiverse cosmology. The
missing philosophical bridges are (i) actuality of the God-possibility
premise, (ii) the necessary-if-existent reading of God rather than arbitrary
contingent deities, (iii) the metaphysical modality governing both claims,
and (iv) return-accessibility or an equivalent route to our standpoint.
Reopen if a particular author's positive modal theory makes one of these
bridges warranted or specifically excludes it; do not treat a mere refusal
to use S5 as a refutation of the weaker back-edge proof.

### Concrete multiverse stress test: causal-entropic selection

Bousso, Harnik, Kribs and Perez, [*Predicting the Cosmological Constant from
the Causal Entropic Principle*](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0702115) (2007),
is an especially useful **positive rival** to stress-test: it predicts a
favored positive cosmological constant under a proposed anthropic weighting,
with the observed value in the favored range. It is not a mere assertion that
`many universes explain anything`. Its introductory decomposition exposes
three **separate** commitments required for that account:

1. An underlying theory admits a sufficiently dense set of vacua/values.
2. Physical dynamics actually populates the relevant alternatives.
3. A sampling/observer measure makes the *observed* value comparatively likely.

In this calculation the third gate is the **causal-entropic principle** (CEP):
weight a causal diamond by entropy produced within it, with stellar radiation
absorbed by dust providing the dominant modeled contribution. The paper's
worked calculation varies **positive cosmological constant** with other
low-energy parameters held fixed. Its result is therefore not a proof that
every combination of physical laws and constants obtains, that every
metaphysical possibility becomes physical, or that the whole ensemble has a
well-founded actuality mechanism. It makes a more valuable, falsifiable-in-
principle conditional proposal with explicit assumptions.

**Exact observer-proxy gap (WP-040–045).** The motivating assertion that
observation needs entropy production licenses, at most, a direction such as
`O > 0 → ΔS > 0`. It does **not** by itself establish the stronger sampling
rule `expected observation-records ∝ ΔS`. A two-candidate *mathematical
surrogate*, not a cosmological counterobservation, proves the distinction:

| Candidate | Prior | Entropy production `ΔS` | Observer records `O` |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| A | 1/2 | 100 | 1 |
| B | 1/2 | 10 | 10 |

All four outcomes are positive, so both candidates satisfy the asserted
necessary connection. Yet entropy weighting selects A with probability
`100/(100+10) = 10/11`, while observer-record weighting selects A with
probability `1/(1+10) = 1/11`. Thus even a fully specified *vacuum
distribution* does not settle a first-person observation likelihood until
the entropy-to-observer proxy is defended independently. The surrogate does
not show that entropy is a bad empirical proxy in our actual cosmology; it
isolates the exact missing implication.

Phillips and Albrecht, [*Effects of Inhomogeneity on the Causal Entropic
Prediction of Λ*](https://arxiv.org/abs/0903.1622) (2009), supply a
**published physical sensitivity test**. Treating an inhomogeneous universe
requires specifying an ensemble and measure over causal diamonds/worldlines;
late entropy from black-hole evaporation can overwhelm stellar entropy under
some choices. Their result makes the original prediction sensitive to a
concrete measure and source-of-entropy choice; it does **not** demonstrate
that no entropy-based or multiverse prediction could succeed. The next
discriminator is whether a physically independently justified weighting
retains the observed-Λ result after these contributions and observer-proxy
assumptions are accounted for.

Nor can we claim that multiverse proponents have *no* developed response to
the measure problem. Khoury and Wong, [*Bayesian Reasoning in Eternal
Inflation: A Solution to the Measure Problem*](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.108.023506)
(2023), derive model-relative comparisons by making ancestral-vacuum and
nucleation-time priors explicit. That is progress on a **different** specified
ensemble; it is not automatically the CEP observer proxy, and the two papers'
assumptions must not be silently spliced together.

**Discriminator and frontier movement.** Ask of each proposed multiverse:
which candidate laws are admissible, why those values are *instantiated*
rather than only mathematically available, what distribution governs them,
what counts as an observation, and how its likelihood responds to alternative
admissible weighting rules. Answering those questions with a concrete,
supported theory genuinely advances the naturalistic case; merely declaring
all combinations actual does not answer them. The symmetrical theistic
comparison must articulate its independently motivated **creation goal and
selection distribution**, condition on observer existence, and predict a
residual feature the natural account does not already favor. Neither bare
`God wanted life` nor bare `there is a multiverse` supplies a Bayes factor.

### PP-06: walk the surviving account one step further

Use `TruthCore.Logic.Scientism` exactly: `established` and
`empiricallyEstablished` are independent predicates. `SCIENTISM_1` requires
empirical establishment for established claims in scope; `SCIENTISM_2`
restricts that requirement by `D.inDomain`. This concerns **epistemic warrant**,
not moral obligation, truth, or cognitive meaningfulness.

The existing result `15a56614-c3f3-59f6-ae7b-52ff9c0f7134`,
[`weakScientismConsistent`](../TruthCore/Results/Logic/Scientism/WeakConsistent/Solution.lean),
has two claims, `false` and `true`. Both are established and in the outer
scope. Only `false` is empirically established and inside the restricted
domain; the policy formulation is `true`. The restricted rule holds, the
policy fails the empirical standard, and the empirical domain is populated.
Thus exclusion of the policy is not a vacuous empty-domain escape. The model
is a predicate interpretation, not a theory of how any real belief acquires
warrant or evidence that a philosopher accepts these predicates.

The positive philosophical replacement is a division of epistemic labor:
empirical methods govern the selected subject matter, while a methodological
policy can receive a different kind of justification. To defend it, the rival
must give a criterion for that subject matter and an account of the policy's
warrant. These are further burdens, not premises discharged by the model.
The strongest parity reply asks whether the purported nonempirical warrant
also qualifies the excluded claim under that very account. It cannot simply
reinstate universal empirical exclusivity, which this rival has surrendered.

Three concrete tests distinguish the branches without another theorem:

1. If the policy lies inside `D`, the existing second-order theorem
   `TruthCore.Results.Logic.Scientism.WeakDemarcation.weakScientismDemarcation`
   applies under its stated outer-scope and nonempirical premises: holding
   the policy excludes its establishment. An author must additionally claim
   establishment before this becomes a clash among endorsed commitments.
2. If the policy lies outside `D` and is established nonempirically, the same
   theorem yields a concession of some nonempirically established claim and
   failure of the stronger scoped rule. That is a genuine commitment of this
   restricted branch, not a refutation of it. No contradiction follows from
   endorsing the restricted rule alone.
3. If the rule is held true but unestablished, the strong dilemma does not
   entail its falsity. Whether it can rationally regulate another belief then
   needs a separate warrant/use premise; the corpus proof does not supply it.

Reject the shortcut from “this policy is nonempirical” to “it refutes itself.”
Also reject the opposite shortcut from the Bool model to “the proposed
restriction is justified.” Verificationist meaning criteria have a different
acceptance predicate and their own analytic/verifiable and scope premises;
`STMT-VERIFICATIONIST-DILEMMA` cannot silently substitute for this warrant test.
