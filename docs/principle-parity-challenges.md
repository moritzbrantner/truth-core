# Principle parity and justified exceptions

This is a **research category**, not a new truth status, source class, inference
rule or proof of any worldview. It collects arguments of the form: *a proponent
applies a standard to one case, but exempts a comparably situated case*. The
important question is whether the standard really has the same meaning and
scope in both cases, and whether a relevant difference explains the treatment.

The `PP-xx` identifiers are stable **intake labels only**. Canonical
propositions remain the existing independent corpus statements; these rows
do not create arguments, graph edges or verified defeats. Related investigations
should reuse the named notes, issues, definitions and Lean results. The
[category issue #1532](https://github.com/moritzbrantner/truth-core/issues/1532)
coordinates follow-ups without replacing their existing owners.

## Test for a genuine parity challenge

1. **Record the opponent's actual commitments.** Name the same proponent,
   the standard `R`, two cases `x,y`, and the accepted/rejected claims.
   Distinguish `R(x)`, `possibly R(x)`, `can do R(x)`, `exists(x)`,
   and `ought accept R(x)`. Do not silently change quantifier, modality,
   time or causal sense between the cases.
2. **Establish relevant comparability.** State what property makes the cases
   alike for `R`. The claim `R(x) ↔ R(y)` is an additional **uniformity
   bridge**, not a consequence of merely calling both entities `things`.
3. **Ask for a positive discriminator.** A challenger proposes that the
   opponent's exception lacks warrant; a defender can defeat the *parity
   inference* with a concrete, norm-relevant difference. Test that difference
   under the same standard for both cases. Do not accept either an arbitrary
   label (`God`, `universe`, `natural`) or an unspecified possible escape
   as a substantive defense.
4. **Report the exact outcome:** a proved inconsistent bundle under stated
   shared principles; a demonstrated relevant difference; a surviving
   conditional challenge; or an unresolved comparison. Even demonstrated
   inconsistency demands revision of **at least one** commitment: it does
   not automatically establish the favored opposite worldview.

An accusation of *special pleading* is thus a question about the **application
of a standard**, not an extra causal, moral or modal premise. The category
overlaps self-application and companions-in-guilt arguments but is broader:
the two compared cases need not be the argument's own assertion or two kinds
of reasons.

## First worked test: universe versus omnipotent being (PP-01)

Let `U` denote a candidate total universe and `G` an omnipotent being.
Different claims must not be collapsed:

- `◇SelfOrigin(U)`: some coherent possible case has a universe causing its
  own *coming-to-be*.
- `Omnipotent(G) → Can(G, SelfOrigin(G))`: an **additional agent/task bridge**.
- `¬∃G Omnipotent(G)`: denial of actual omnipotent beings, which does **not**
  contradict either counterfactual conditional above.
- `¬◇SelfOrigin(G)`: a **modal impossibility** claim, much stronger than
  nonexistence, whose justification can be compared with `◇SelfOrigin(U)`.

The live target is the **relevant-difference premise**: if the critic grants
actual productive self-origination for `U` but denies even its possibility
for `G`, which difference explains that verdict? The serious proposed
distinctions are a composite versus an agent, reciprocal creation of parts
versus an act borne by the whole, and contingent beginning versus necessary
existence. Smith/Newman's simultaneous-parts universe targets *beginning*,
not merely event recurrence; the positive actual-source principle from
the [origin note](notes/unique-feedback-explanation-311.md) must be tested
against it without begging priority or inventing a temporal requirement.

**Provisional judgment (2026-11-12).** The bare argument “atheists deny an
omnipotent being, so they must deny self-origination” is invalid because
actual nonexistence is not modal impossibility. The *same-causal-standard*
challenge is legitimate and worth investigating, but its bridge from a
universe's internal mutual causation to a hypothetical agent's
self-originating **ability** is OPEN. Nothing here proves the no-self-origin
premise, the existence of God, or inconsistency in atheism as such.

Reversal conditions: a worked account of the same origin relation in both
cases with no norm-relevant discriminator would strengthen the parity
criticism; a justified difference that survives equal application would
defeat that particular parity inference without establishing a general
causal prohibition.

atheism in general. It tests several *different* positive proposals rather than

establishing it is physically possible, and giving reason to think it is

### The strongest new discriminator: reasons for choosing an explanatory stop

Suppose two accounts each put one purportedly necessary source at the base:
`N` is an impersonal natural initial structure and `G` is a necessary personal
source. This pair shares a *formal stopping position*, but three discriminators
are still open and **not equivalent**:

1. **Actuality/necessity:** What warrants the metaphysical necessity of this
   *bearer*, as opposed to mere necessity of there being *some* initial state?
   Oppy's permitted-world restriction is a positive proposal; merely declaring
   the actual initial singularity necessary is not an argument for that
   restriction. A claim that God is necessary likewise needs more than the word
   `God`. Compare the de re/description controls already under #468/#537.
2. **Selection/actual contingent detail:** Does the source genuinely account
   for why this contingent history and its laws obtain, or simply precede it
   in the model? Oppy counts a single natural and a single supernatural cause
   as equal on *general causal explanation*; that comparison leaves this
   stronger explanatory demand untested. A divine choice introduces purpose
   and intention but then owes the connection from purpose to **this** choice;
   a necessarily given source need not necessitate its contingent effects.
3. **Causal category and self-application:** Does a purported ability to
   originate require an already actual power-bearing subject? If yes, apply
   this to the first constituent event of a self-originating loop **and** to an
   act of divine creation; the latter does not purport to bring the already
   existent divine subject into being. If no, identify the actual originating
   relation rather than substituting counterfactual dependence, mutual
   event-causation or a global fixed point for productive existence.

**The label-erasure test.** Oppy's comparative cost counts a natural-only
causal ontology as one kind of cause, versus natural *plus* non-natural as
two kinds (§IV). This is not wrong just because the terms differ. To check
its explanatory relevance, replace the labels with independently specified
properties and causal roles. If *natural* simply means *whatever causes*,
the one-kind advantage risks being verbal and admits any hypothesized creator
as a causal relatum. If it instead means a substantive physical/impersonal
kind, the source needs a positive argument that this kind can be the ultimate
producer of this order; the two-kind accounting must also consider what
further laws or primitive selection facts the models require. Likewise a
term defined as *God* cannot acquire productive powers, necessity or
goodness just from its spelling. This is a proposed discriminator test, not
an attribution that Oppy *does* define naturality as causality alone.

**A concrete logical warning.** `□P` alone does **not** entail `¬Caused(P)`:
consider two admissible worlds, both with `P` and `Q` obtaining, and in both
an irreflexive causal/grounding arrow `Q → P`. One may deny this model by
adopting a definition of causation requiring modal variation in the effect;
that is the *additional bridge* to defend. Oppy's §VI inference from necessary
obtaining to causal independence must not be adopted as a logical axiom just
because it appears in a source. Conversely this counterexample does not
establish any **real** causal relation among necessities.

**Priority investigation, not a finding of defeat.** The strongest direct
target is the claim of *equal explanatory breadth/depth* under Oppy's
theoretical-virtue comparison. Ask specifically whether the same model
answers (a) why anything exists, (b) why these contingent laws/conditions
obtain, and (c) why the source has productive power, with the same standards
in both natural and divine branches. The second target is Wielenberg's
explicitly brute necessary moral supervenience versus a positive,
nondefinitional theistic account of authority. No global naturalist/theistic
refutation or new corpus closure follows from this audit.

**Do not combine incompatible rescue moves.** Oppy's 2020 account explicitly
rejects self-causes and causal circles; Newman argues that some causal loops
are directly self-caused. They are individually assessable alternatives, **not**
two premises a single naturalist can invoke together without revision. In a
debate, preserve each opponent's chosen causal theory, modal accessibility,
source type and explanatory criterion across the entire branch. Switching
theories to meet successive challenges is an *alternative-model search*;
the union of its individually successful replies is not one demonstrated
coherent and adequate explanation. The same discipline applies to shifting
among different theistic theories of causation, freedom and divine necessity.

## Integration rule

Promote an item only when it has a meaning-audited canonical statement, an
explicit inference/argument bundle if a logical step is claimed, and any
required verified proof or model. Record rivals and rejected moves in the
existing target's attempt/issue/note; do not create a second category
database. [The PP-01 exploration map](../explorations/principle-parity-self-origination.toml)
tracks its first concrete comparison as a **mapped, uncertified rival**, not
an implication from parity to the no-self-origin conclusion. No new root
premise is marked established by this inventory.
