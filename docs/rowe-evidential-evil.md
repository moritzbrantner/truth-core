# Rowe’s evidential challenge and the grounding boundary

Issue #168, following #150/#160/#149. This is a scoped reading of William L.
Rowe, “The Problem of Evil and Some Varieties of Atheism,” *American
Philosophical Quarterly* 16(4) (1979), 335–341. The primary university-hosted
journal scan and byte-identical fixed 2016-09-09 Wayback capture are pinned in
`ROWE-EVIL-ATHEISM-1979-FULL`; the entire article was materialized and read.
Printed pp.336–339 carry the argument and reply. The PDF’s two-column extraction

## What the argument actually challenges

Rowe distinguishes narrow theism (a being jointly omniscient, omnipotent and
wholly good) from broader deity claims (p.335). His premises on p.336 are:

1. Some actual intense suffering could be prevented by an omniscient omnipotent
   being without unavoidable loss of a greater good or permission of equal or
   worse evil.
2. A wholly good omniscient being permits preventable intense suffering only
   under such a cost of prevention.

The conclusion is the denial of narrow theism. `POE-ROWE-DEDUCTIVE-CORE`
mechanically derives that consequence by instantiating the suffering witness
at a supposed being with all three attributes: the first premise excludes the
cost which the second requires. Costs have an outcome carrier and separate
counterfactual and comparative relations; the proof does not simply apply an
opaque implication with atheism already as its consequent. Neither premise is
proved true, and no Catholic root premise is discharged.

Rowe’s second premise is **necessary**, not sufficient, for morally permissible
permission (pp.336–337). Merely bringing some good out of suffering, or outweighing
it, need not suffice. Thus the existing Catholic general-evil consistency model
and q.2 a.3 ad 1’s `GodDrawsGoodFromEveryEvilReading` do not answer this argument:
that relation establishes neither unavoidable loss, unreplaceability, comparative
weight, nor an account of each actual suffering. The separate q.48 a.5 culpa cell
(#450) classifies voluntary operational defects conditionally; it explains neither
natural suffering nor divine permission.

The formal reading preserves two qualifications explicitly rather than quietly
strengthening the text:

- Footnote 2 excludes replaceable conjunctive goods (a good plus the suffering
  cannot manufacture necessity when the good alone is available). `GreaterGood`
  has this intended restriction; its truth in a real case is open.
- Footnote 3 allows justice as a good and imposes no later-in-time restriction.
  It also considers goods whose loss leaves the balance unchanged, admitting
  that the printed premise is somewhat too strong without this refinement.
  `POE-ROWE-BALANCE-NEUTRAL-CORE` also instantiates the proved structured core
  with this proposed refinement. It weakens the necessary permission constraint
  and correspondingly strengthens the factual gratuitous-suffering premise;
  both remain explicit and open. It neither proves the two formulations
  equivalent nor attributes the stronger simplified condition to every theist.

## Appearance, actuality and epistemic warrant

The fawn case (p.337) motivates the difference between lacking a recognized
justifying connection and knowing that no such connection exists. Rowe explicitly
accepts that appearance alone does not entail his first premise. His affirmative
case then invokes the range and profusion of suffering, its apparently weak
connection to goods, and the apparent availability of less suffering even when
some good is involved (pp.337–338, especially n.5). The strongest claim in this
article is defeasible rational support for premise (1), including support for
acceptance over suspension, rather than a demonstration or a numerical
probability theorem.

`ApparentlyPointlessSuffering` is only a limited finite-investigator projection
of the first appearance claim. It omits the distribution of suffering, background
information and rational warrant. `POE-ROWE-APPARENT-COMPATIBILITY` gives a concrete
finite interpretation with actual intense suffering, a qualifying theistic being,
an unrecognized unavoidable greater-good cost, and the necessary permission
constraint. It reuses the deductive-core result to exclude actual gratuitous
suffering in that interpretation. This establishes logical compatibility of this
limited appearance with narrow theism. It provides no probability, likelihood,
actual possibility, actual greater good, or sufficient moral license. In
particular, it is not a refutation of Rowe’s inductive argument.

The open warrant obligation is to specify which total evidence and epistemic
principles license moving from appearances to actual gratuitous suffering. A
proof that the inference is not deductively valid does not discharge it. No
probability premise is silently assumed or replaced by an opaque warrant bridge.
The candidate `ROWE-EVIL-TOTAL-EVIDENCE-WARRANT` preserves this gap.

## Shared answer map and the owner’s dilemma

The question remains `Q-MORAL-GROUNDING`: what makes the evaluative judgments
objective? The map is stored in these scoped argument/definition records and this
linked note, following #149’s existing set-of-propositions arrangement; no new
storage schema is introduced.

| Expressive/noncognitivist external argument | Existing emotivism result applies only when its exact moral-classification and truth/semantic premises are supplied | No Rowe-to-O’Connor sentence bridge or attribution is established here; descriptive suffering can remain true |
| Internal critic | Grant the theist’s evaluative standards conditionally and challenge permission under those standards | The critic need not adopt an independently grounded moral realism to pose the conditional inconsistency |

Applied conditionally, the owner’s dilemma says: if an external argument asserts
an objective evaluative premise, its complete rival worldview owes a grounding
answer; if it denies that very premise’s truth-aptness, it cannot simultaneously
use it as an objectively true external premise under the same semantics. This is
an obligation or a conditional inconsistency, not an automatic refutation of
Rowe. Merely lacking a grounding answer in this article is unexplained, not false.
Nor is an independent realist account refuted by its being non-theistic.

Keep four items separate: a natural suffering event; a voluntarily controlled
fault; an objective evaluative or permission judgment; and an expression of an
attitude. The existing theorem
`externalPremiseContradictsEmotivism` concerns a classified man-made-evil sentence
and explicit truth-aptness premises. None of its semantic bridges can be imported
merely because Rowe uses the word “evil.” His permission principle is the primary
evaluative pressure point (p.337 calls it a basic moral principle); the forest
fire’s occurrence by itself is not a voluntary fault. A hypothetical/internal
critique also survives the critic’s refusal to endorse objective morality as
an independent external commitment.

## Strong replies preserved, without resolving them by stipulation

Rowe’s own strongest theistic reply is the Moorean shift (pp.338–339): a believer
may accept the permission constraint, have independent rational grounds for a
theistic being, and consequently reject actual gratuitous suffering. Its logic
is the same cost contradiction used by the present theorem. Its epistemic force
depends on comparing those independent grounds with the suffering evidence, not
on declaring either first premise true. Rowe also distinguishes challenging the
sufficiency of the atheistic evidence from producing a specific actual good for
every event (p.338). His friendly atheism (pp.340–341) explicitly allows some
theists to be rationally justified while denying their belief’s truth.

As an open skeptical-theist reply, one can challenge whether finite investigators
should expect to recognize all morally relevant prevention costs. The non-theist
can answer that ordinary defeasible induction does not demand omniscience, that
the large distribution matters more than a single opaque case, and that possible
hidden goods alone do not establish such goods or erase the evidence. Broad
skepticism also owes an account of why everyday moral deliberation remains
reliable. These are preserved philosophical alternatives, not newly attributed
primary-source claims about later authors. No proposition about epistemic access,
priors, likelihoods, moral paralysis or total evidence is proved by the finite
model. A full comparison must keep these premises explicit.

## Premises and root effects

Intended and actual: no root-route change. The deductive result retains exactly
the actual-gratuitous-suffering premise and simplified necessary permission
constraint. It adds no third philosophical premise and removes neither. The refined
argument replaces both simplified premises together with their explicitly
qualified counterparts; it does not discharge either counterpart. The
existential model has no theorem hypotheses; its four recorded commitments are
satisfied only by the displayed interpretation. It establishes no premise about
the actual world. The source records establish what was read, not premise truth.
Issue #168 remains open for its other obligations and for the total-evidence warrant
recorded here. The six unavailable O’Connor exports are
untouched.
